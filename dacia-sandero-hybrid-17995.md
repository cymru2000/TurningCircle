Dacia opened UK orders for the Sandero hybrid 155 today, priced from £17,995 on the road. That is £2,000 below the MG3 Hybrid+, which had been the cheapest full hybrid on sale in Britain, and £2,230 above the petrol Sandero Expression.

At average mileage the fuel saving is £273 a year, so recovering the premium takes a little over eight years.

## What the range costs

The hatch comes in Expression at £17,995 and Journey at £19,295. The raised Stepway body is £19,295 in Expression and £20,595 in Extreme. Those on-the-road prices include £365 of first-year vehicle tax, £700 delivery and the £55 first registration fee, [according to Dacia's pricing table](https://www.press.dacia.co.uk/releases/583).

The gap to petrol is £2,230 in both Sandero trims. The petrol Expression lists at £15,765 and the petrol Journey at £17,065 in [Dacia's December 2025 price announcement](https://www.press.dacia.co.uk/releases/539), and the hybrid sits exactly £2,230 above each. Stepway buyers pay the same premium over the equivalent TCe 110.

## The powertrain in plain numbers

A 1.8-litre four-cylinder petrol engine producing 80kW, a 36kW traction motor and a 1.4kWh battery add up to 155hp. The clutchless gearbox has four ratios for the engine and two for the motor, and chooses between them itself.

The 0-62mph sprint takes 8.3 seconds, against 9.7 for the petrol TCe 100, [Auto Express reports](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/dacia/sandero/365595/new-dacia-sandero-hybrid-takes-uks-cheapest-hybrid-crown). Combined economy is 67mpg for the Sandero and 64mpg for the Stepway, against 52.3mpg for the TCe 100. The car always starts in electric mode and can stay there for up to 80% of urban driving time. The boot holds 410 litres, the same as the petrol version, and the braked towing limit is 1,100kg.

Lina Ribeiro, Dacia Brand Director for the UK, said: "Sandero has always been about giving customers more of what they actually use, without making them pay for things they don't. Hybrid 155 fits that thinking perfectly. It brings automatic driving, electric power around town and lower fuel consumption, but there's nothing extra to plug in or worry about."

## The £2,230 question

Fuel is the only place the hybrid earns money back. The government's weekly road fuel figures put the average petrol price at [172.78p a litre in the week to 2 November](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/weekly-road-fuel-prices). At that price, a petrol Sandero covering 8,296 miles a year, the average for a petrol car in Solera Cap HPI's trade data, [as we set out in August](/how-far-do-uk-drivers-drive/), burns 721 litres and costs £1,246. The hybrid burns 563 litres and costs £973.

| Annual mileage | Fuel saved | Annual saving | Years to repay £2,230 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5,000 | 95 litres | £165 | 13.5 |
| 8,296 | 158 litres | £273 | 8.2 |
| 10,000 | 191 litres | £330 | 6.8 |
| 12,000 | 229 litres | £395 | 5.6 |
| 15,000 | 286 litres | £494 | 4.5 |

These are fuel-only figures, using official combined consumption that few drivers match. Dacia's release claims consumption "up to 40% lower than an equivalent petrol engine". On the combined cycle the gap between the two Sandero engines is 22% less fuel per mile, or 28% more miles per gallon, so the 40% depends on the urban conditions where the battery does most of the work.

## The only automatic in the range

The UK Sandero range is manual only in petrol form, so the hybrid is the only two-pedal version Dacia sells here. Part of the £2,230 pays for the battery and motors, and part pays for a gearbox that is not available at any lower price. A buyer who needs an automatic is not comparing like with like against the £15,765 manual.

The MG3 Hybrid+ starts at £19,995 in SE trim, [according to MG's own pricing](https://www.mg.co.uk/media-centre/mg-upgrades-customer-favourites-smoother-hybrid-driving-and-greater-comfort), so the Sandero is £2,000 cheaper for the same layout of engine, electric motor and self-charging battery.

## Plug-ins have overtaken self-charging hybrids

[SMMT's registration tables](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/car-registrations/) for the first nine months of 2026 show 240,255 self-charging hybrids registered, up 7.9% on last year but down from a 14.11% market share to 13.81%. Plug-in hybrids reached 244,769, up 41.8%, and their share rose from 10.94% to 14.07%. Battery electric cars took 26.16%, up from 22.14%.

Plug-ins have therefore overtaken self-charging hybrids in volume, by 4,514 cars. The market grew 10.2% to 1,739,271, so a powertrain growing at 7.9% is losing share even while its numbers rise. Our [guide to hybrid sales in 2026](/hybrid-sales-2026/) traced why plug-ins have outgrown everything else this year.

## The electric option is £6,005 cheaper

Dacia also sells the Spring, which [starts at £11,990](https://www.dacia.co.uk/hybrid-and-electric-range/spring-city-car.html) and covers 140 miles on the WLTP combined cycle. It is a much smaller car with a 308-litre boot.

For a buyer who can charge at home and rarely drives more than 80 miles in a day, it costs £6,005 less to buy and considerably less to run, a case our [comparison of small EVs and city driving](/small-evs-city-driving/) sets out. The hybrid makes sense for the buyer who cannot charge at home, or who regularly covers distances that would need a charging stop.

## What it means for a buyer

Annual mileage decides most of it. Below about 6,000 miles a year the petrol Sandero is the cheaper way to do the same job, and above 12,000 the hybrid starts to make sense on fuel alone. If you need an automatic, the premium is doing two jobs at once. If you can charge at home, the Spring costs less to buy and to run.

The used market is watching the same powertrain: [used hybrid stock is closing on 100,000 cars](/used-hybrid-stock-100000/).

The Sandero hybrid is a practical five-seat supermini with a proven powertrain, a decent boot and a price that no rival full hybrid matches. Whether the extra is worth paying depends on how you use the car, not on the economy figure alone.
