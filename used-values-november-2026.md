Average used car trade values at three years and 60,000 miles fell 3.5% in November, according to [Solera cap hpi's monthly market read](https://motortrader.com/motor-trader-news/automotive-news/solera-cap-hpi-reports-fall-in-used-car-values-26-11-2026), published today. The typical fall at this point in the year is around 2%, so this month's movement is nearly double the seasonal norm. It follows a 1.7% drop in October, and it means values have now fallen in every month of 2026 so far.

The fall is not evenly spread. Older, higher-mileage cars took the biggest hits, while the newest used stock held up best, and battery electric cars again lost less than every other fuel type.

## The November numbers by age

Cap hpi's benchmark is a car at three years old with 60,000 miles, and that profile fell 3.5% this month. Around it, the age and mileage profile tells the same story: the older the car, the harder it fell.

- One year and 10,000 miles: down 2.6%, about £890
- Three years and 60,000 miles: down 3.5%
- Five years and 80,000 miles: down 4.6%, about £590
- Ten years and 100,000 miles: down 5%, about £240

At the three-year benchmark, diesel cars fell furthest, down 4.6% or roughly £1,000, followed by petrol at 4.3% (£790), plug-in hybrids at 3.4% (£900) and hybrids at 2.8% (£460). Battery electric cars fell just 0.9%, about £265, making them the most resilient fuel type of the month by a clear margin. At the ten-year, 100,000-mile point the contrast was even starker: electric values rose 1.1% while the market average fell 5%.

## Why the market turned sharper this month

Rising stock levels and weaker consumer demand are the two forces behind the fall, according to Solera cap hpi. Dealers have more cars to choose from at auction and are being far more selective about what they buy, while preparation costs and pressure on retail margins make older cars that need work before sale particularly hard to shift.

Jeremy Yea, senior valuations editor at Solera cap hpi, said: "We would expect values to come under pressure at this time of year, but the scale of this month's fall goes beyond normal trends. Dealers have plenty of stock to choose from and are being much more selective about what they buy. With preparation costs and pressure on retail margins, older cars that need work before they can go on the forecourt are proving particularly difficult to sell."

The supply side is building through the final quarter. September's plate change produced a wave of part-exchange cars, and fleet returns are adding to wholesale volumes, so the extra stock is arriving at exactly the time of year when demand normally softens.

## Retail prices are holding up better than trade values

The trade market is softer than the forecourt. Autotrader's October [Retail Price Index](https://plc.autotrader.co.uk/news-views/press-releases/used-car-retail-prices-hold-steady-month-on-month-as-market-continues-to-show-resilience), published on 9 November, shows average advertised used car prices flat month on month at £17,321, down 1.8% on a year earlier, with cars taking an average of 29 days to sell, unchanged from October 2025. Electric cars recorded a sixth consecutive month of annual price growth, up 2.8%.

At auction, [BCA's October figures](https://www.bca.co.uk/news/used-car-sector-under-pressure-from-continuing-headwinds-20261104/) put average used car values at £7,244, down £73, or 1.0%, on September. The gap between retail asking prices and trade values is where dealers' margins sit, and a widening gap is what Yea is warning about when he says buyers are being more selective.

## What December looks like

Solera cap hpi expects further falls in December as more stock reaches the wholesale market and dealers work towards year-end targets. Yea does not expect a January revival: "There will be dealers hoping for the traditional pick-up in business in January, but given the amount of stock in the market, an improvement in demand is more likely to slow the rate of depreciation than lead to an increase in values. There are still buyers for good-quality used cars, but dealers need to keep a close eye on pricing and stock turnover in the coming weeks."

For context, this is the eleventh consecutive November in which values have fallen. Since cap hpi launched its Cap Live data in 2012, values at the three-year benchmark have fallen in 13 of the 14 Novembers, with 2021 the only rise.

## What it means if you are buying or selling

For sellers, the message is to price realistically against current stock rather than last month's book. The [October read of the same data](/used-values-october-2026/) showed values down 1.7% with electric cars the only fuel type to rise, and November has widened the gap between the newest and oldest stock rather than changing the direction. Anyone holding a three-to-five-year-old car should expect the trade to discount for the volume now coming through, and our [September analysis](/used-ev-september-values/) shows how the fuel-type split has moved month to month this year.

For buyers, a softer trade market usually means better deals on the forecourt within weeks, particularly on older, higher-mileage cars where the falls are largest. The electric exception cuts both ways: battery electric values fell least again in November, which supports resale prices for sellers but means less of a discount for buyers. With advertised stock from Chinese brands now past 30,000 cars, as [our recent analysis](/used-chinese-cars-record-30000-forecourts/) covered, competition for used buyers' attention is only increasing.

The trade-versus-retail split is worth watching through December. If Autotrader's advertised prices start following cap hpi's trade values down, the softening will have reached the forecourt; if they hold flat as they did in October, dealers are absorbing the fall in margin instead.
