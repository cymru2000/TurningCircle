Great Wall Motor (GWM) will take direct control of its UK passenger car business from the second quarter of 2027, setting up its own national sales company and ending the distributor-led model that has carried the brand here since its 2022 launch. The announcement comes after a run of weak registration figures: SMMT data shows GWM registered just [163 cars in the UK in the first ten months of 2026](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/car-registrations/), down 63% on the 439 recorded in the same period of 2025, in a market that grew 9.8% over those ten months.

International Motors Limited (IML), which helped establish GWM in the UK, will keep the brand's utility vehicle business, including the POER300 pick-up. Passenger cars will pass to the new company. Country manager Steve Ma said the move would let GWM "build a stronger local organisation, work more closely with our retail partners and customers, and support the introduction of a broader passenger vehicle portfolio".

## The numbers behind the switch

GWM's UK sales have been shrinking for several years. The Ora 03, originally launched as the Ora Funky Cat in November 2022, was [withdrawn from UK sale in April 2026](https://www.electrive.com/2026/04/25/gwm-discontinues-ora-03-electric-hatchback-in-the-uk) after registering 542 cars in 2025 and only 26 in the first quarter of this year. October brought just [two GWM registrations, against 20 in October 2025](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/car-registrations/), while the overall market rose [5.4% to 152,730](https://www.smmt.co.uk/year-end-push-lifts-ev-sales-but-mandate-gap-set-to-widen) in its eleventh consecutive month of growth.

Across 2025 as a whole, GWM's three UK brands, Ora, Haval and Poer, [collectively registered 629 units across 26 dealer sites](https://www.motortrader.com/motor-trader-news/automotive-news/chinese-cars-the-big-push-25-03-2026), a figure Motor Trader described as a period of regrouping after "an early but ultimately less than successful start in the UK in 2022". The POER300 pick-up, launched in September 2025 from £31,495 CVOTR, has fared little better, with [25 UK registrations in its first full year](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/features/368130/what-gwm-poer-complete-brand-model-and-tech-guide-uk-car-buyers).

## What stays with International Motors

The split leaves IML in charge of GWM's utility vehicles, the POER300 pick-up among them. Passenger cars, which today means the Haval Jolion Pro hybrid SUV, will move to the new national sales company when it opens in Q2 2027. GWM has not yet said which of its European passenger models will come to the UK, their specifications or their launch dates.

The Jolion Pro launched here in February 2025 from £23,995 and is [now listed from £27,995](https://gwmcars.co.uk/) with a £2,000 GWM Hybrid Grant for retail customers registering by 31 December 2026. The POER300 is [currently listed from £38,911 CVOTR](https://gwmcars.co.uk/models/poer300/). GWM's UK operation had more than 46 locations when it outlined its network plans last year, with ambitions for [100 sales and service centres plus 40 aftersales points over five years](https://www.am-online.com/news/gwm-sets-out-european-expansion-plan-with-new-models-and-mixed-distribution-strategy).

IML managing director William Brown said the agreement "represents a natural progression in our partnership with GWM and comes at the right time for both businesses", adding that IML looks forward to "developing the GWM utility vehicle business".

## The European backdrop and the Bulgaria plant

The UK switch is part of GWM's wider European expansion, which has seen the company enter Spain, Italy and Poland and set up its own sales companies in the larger markets. It is also investing in local manufacturing: production of the Ora 5 compact SUV is due to start at a plant in Bulgaria in [the first quarter of 2027, with planned annual capacity of 50,000 vehicles](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/gwm-to-begin-ora-5-production-in-bulgaria-from-2027/) under a manufacturing agreement with local partner Bulgarian Automotive Industry.

That investment is aimed at a weak European record. Reuters reported that registrations of GWM's Ora EV brand across Europe [fell 41% to 3,706 vehicles in 2024](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/chinas-gwm-targets-300000-annual-production-with-first-europe-car-plant-2025-11-26/), and the company has said it targets annual European production of 300,000 vehicles by 2029. Building cars inside the EU also keeps them clear of the bloc's anti-subsidy duties on Chinese-made EVs.

## The Xpeng precedent

GWM is not the first Chinese brand to drop the distributor model in Britain. Xpeng [established its own UK national sales company on 1 September 2026](https://xpengcars.co.uk/2026/09/xpeng-strengthens-uk-presence-with-new-direct-operating-structure), taking over from IML while drawing on its operational infrastructure, and is now building towards around 100 outlets by the end of 2027 as covered in [our recent piece on its L03 and G9L pricing](/xpeng-uk-network-100-l03-g9l/). A direct structure gives the manufacturer tighter control of pricing, stock and brand presentation, which matters in a market where [Chinese brands took almost a quarter of UK registrations in September](/chinese-brands-record-share/) and are still adding share.

## What it means for UK buyers

For now, nothing changes on the forecourt. The Jolion Pro hybrid and the POER300 diesel pick-up remain on sale through the existing network, and GWM says aftersales support for cars already sold continues as before. The practical question is what the new sales company brings to the UK. GWM's European passenger range spans petrol, hybrid, plug-in hybrid and fully electric powertrains across its Ora, Haval, Wey and Tank lines, so the in-house structure could open the door to models the distributor model never carried, as [we set out when the Chinese brands arrived in force](/chinese-brands-uk-arrival/).

Notably, GWM's current UK line-up is hybrid and diesel only, which leaves it outside any electric-only tariff on Chinese-built cars that Britain might adopt in future. Whether that insulation lasts depends on which powertrains the new company chooses to bring here, and those plans have yet to be announced.
