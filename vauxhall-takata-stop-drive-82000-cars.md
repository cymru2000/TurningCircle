Vauxhall has told owners of five older model lines to stop driving until their Takata airbags are replaced. The stop-drive covers the Astra H and Astravan from 2005 to 2013, the Astra Twin Top from 2005 to 2012, and the Vectra and Signum from 2005 to 2008.

The five lines sit inside a wider recall that was published in December 2025 and lists 949,336 vehicle records across eight Vauxhall models. Up to 82,000 cars still have an outstanding repair, according to Vauxhall, and owners of the five are being told to stop using the car, with one exception: a direct journey to a pre-booked repair.

## Five model lines, one instruction

The stop-drive was issued by Stellantis UK, Vauxhall's parent company, and applies to cars that were already part of the Takata recall. Stellantis says the decision followed a review of the latest monitoring data and the ageing characteristics of the inflators, and that there have been no reported instances in the UK ([Vauxhall](https://www.vauxhall.co.uk/owners/maintenance-and-repair/takata-airbag-recall.html)).

The instruction is blunt. "Stellantis urges all affected vehicle owners to stop driving their vehicle as soon as they receive a stop-drive notification and to contact their authorised retailer immediately to arrange for the free repair," Stellantis said ([Express, 4 November](https://www.express.co.uk/life-style/cars/2263226/vauxhall-car-recall-notice-airbag)). Owners may drive the car directly to the repairer, and nowhere else.

The other Vauxhall models in the recall, the Astra J, Mokka and Mokka X, Meriva B, Cascada and Zafira C, are not under the stop-drive order, though they still need their airbags replaced ([Newsquest, 6 November](https://www.echo-news.co.uk/news/26707261.thousands-vauxhall-owners-told-stop-driving-due-issue/)).

Vauxhall is running the wider recall in phases rather than in one go ([Vauxhall](https://www.vauxhall.co.uk/owners/maintenance-and-repair/takata-airbag-recall.html)). The stop-drive sits outside that schedule: it applies to any car in the five lines where the repair has not already been carried out.

## Why an old inflator is the dangerous part

Takata inflators use a chemical propellant that can degrade over years of heat and humidity. Vauxhall's warning says the inflator can "explode with excessive force" in a crash, which can cause serious injury or death ([Vauxhall](https://www.vauxhall.co.uk/owners/maintenance-and-repair/takata-airbag-recall.html)). The DVSA notice uses similar terms: the inflator may rupture with too much force, and the remedy is to replace the driver's airbag module ([DVSA R/2025/511, 1 December 2025](https://carsmultiverse.com/uk-recall/r-2025-511/)).

That is why this is a stop-drive rather than a warning to book a service. A software update cannot make the part safe. The airbag unit has to be removed and replaced.

## The 82,000 cars that have not been fixed

The recall was published on 1 December 2025 and covers the Astra H, Astra J, Mokka, Meriva B, Cascada, Signum, Vectra C and Zafira C ([DVSA R/2025/511](https://carsmultiverse.com/uk-recall/r-2025-511/)). Vauxhall says it has written to registered keepers several times. It told the Express that up to 82,000 cars still have an outstanding safety recall ([Express, 4 November](https://www.express.co.uk/life-style/cars/2263226/vauxhall-car-recall-notice-airbag)).

Stellantis has replaced the Takata airbags in more than four million of its vehicles across Europe by the end of August 2026 ([Express, 4 November](https://www.express.co.uk/life-style/cars/2263226/vauxhall-car-recall-notice-airbag)). The cars that remain are often the ones that have changed hands or moved address.

Recall letters go to the registered keeper at the address held by the DVLA. A house move or a private sale can break that chain, and so can a car that has been off the road for a while. Vauxhall asks owners who have changed address since buying the car to update their details with the DVLA so the letter reaches them ([Vauxhall](https://www.vauxhall.co.uk/owners/maintenance-and-repair/takata-airbag-recall.html)).

The DVSA record shows the spread of the campaign. It lists 231,159 Astra H, 54,365 Vectra C and 333 Signum, alongside the Astra J, Mokka, Meriva B, Cascada and Zafira C ([DVSA R/2025/511](https://carsmultiverse.com/uk-recall/r-2025-511/)). The Astra H is the big one, and it is also the model most likely to have passed through several owners by now.

Bond Turner, a law firm, says it is investigating potential claims over motorists' losses, including replacement vehicle hire and lost earnings ([Newsquest, 6 November](https://www.echo-news.co.uk/news/26707261.thousands-vauxhall-owners-told-stop-driving-due-issue/)). The repair itself remains free, whether the car was bought new or used ([Vauxhall](https://www.vauxhall.co.uk/owners/maintenance-and-repair/takata-airbag-recall.html)).

## How to check a VIN and book the repair

The VIN is the 17-character code on the V5C registration certificate and at the base of the windscreen. Vauxhall's recall checker takes the VIN and says whether the car is in the campaign and what to do next ([Vauxhall](https://www.vauxhall.co.uk/owners/maintenance-and-repair/vehicle-recall-check.html)). Owners who cannot use the website can call the recall helpline on 0800 917 9285.

The repair is free at an authorised Vauxhall retailer. The campaign code is KBM and the manufacturer reference is 221044CO, with the DVSA reference R/2025/511 ([DVSA R/2025/511](https://carsmultiverse.com/uk-recall/r-2025-511/)). GOV.UK says drivers do not usually have to pay for repairs or parts under a safety recall ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vehicle-recalls-and-faults)). The work replaces the driver's airbag module.

## Before buying one at the cheap end

These are now cheap used cars, and that is the market where a recall is easiest to miss. A private seller may not know the car is subject to a stop-drive, and an MOT certificate will not show it. The MOT tests the car on the day and does not check whether a manufacturer recall has been completed.

Anyone buying an Astra H, Astravan, Twin Top, Vectra or Signum should check the VIN before agreeing a price, and should not drive the car away if it is under the order. The repair stays with the car, so a completed replacement is a point in the seller's favour.

The wider used market has been soft. The average car sold at BCA's auctions fetched £7,244 in October, 5% less than a year earlier ([AM Online, 6 November](/auction-values-october-2026/)). Ask the seller for proof that the replacement has been done, since the repair costs nothing and the record should be easy to produce.

## A recall that reaches beyond Vauxhall

Takata airbags were fitted across the industry, and the DVSA file carries Takata campaigns for BMW, Ford, Honda, Mercedes-Benz, Volkswagen and others ([recall directory](https://www.vehiclerecallcheck.co.uk/takata-airbags/)). The Vauxhall campaign alone lists 949,336 vehicle records.

Recalls are not confined to older cars. This week Volkswagen capped the charging of about 5,000 UK electric cars at 80% while it inspects batteries that could overheat ([TurningCircle](/volkswagen-id-battery-recall-80-percent-cap/)). The difference is the remedy: Volkswagen's cars can still be driven with a reduced charge, while Vauxhall's five model lines are meant to stay parked until the airbag is replaced.
