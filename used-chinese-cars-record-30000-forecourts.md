More than 30,000 used cars from Chinese brands were advertised for sale in the UK in October, a record high and a 10% rise on September, according to retail data from vehicle valuation specialist Cazana. Every month of 2026 has brought a fresh increase, and over 25,000 cars were already listed on retailer websites by mid-November. The growing stock is beginning to show in prices: across MG, BYD, Changan, Chery and Jaecoo, advertised values of 25-plate cars fell by around 0.5% in the first three weeks of November.

## Who is on the forecourt

Cazana tracked dealer advertisements for cars four years old or younger, on 22-plates or newer, across ten brands: BYD, Changan, Chery, Geely, GWM, Jaecoo, MG, Omoda, Skywell and Xpeng. MG dominates the used stock with roughly 14,000 advertised cars, a position built on more than 85,000 new registrations in 2025. BYD, which registered just 1,600 cars in the UK in 2023 before reaching 51,000 in 2025, now has close to 4,000 used cars advertised on 22-plates and newer. Omoda and Jaecoo, which entered the UK in 2024 with 48,000 combined registrations in 2025, had passed 99,494 combined new registrations by the end of October this year, with around 1,000 used Omodas and 1,800 used Jaecoos advertised. Chery had registered around 37,000 new cars by the end of October with about 1,600 used listings, and Geely close to 700.

These are retail figures only. Cazana excludes stock circulating in wholesale and trade channels, so the true volume available for resale is considerably higher. The new-car side of the same story is in [the SMMT's year-to-date marque table](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/car-registrations/), which puts the ten Chinese brands' combined registrations at 337,730 for the first ten months of 2026, 17.9% of the 1,892,000-car market, against 8.8% a year earlier. MG leads the group on 86,767, ahead of BYD on 74,470, Jaecoo on 64,825, Chery on 37,071 and Omoda on 34,669.

## Why near-new prices are slipping

Values of three-year-old Chinese-brand cars are holding steady, Cazana says, largely because relatively few cars of that age have yet reached the market. The pressure is on newer stock. Dealers were advertising around 6,000 MGs, 3,500 BYDs and 1,700 Jaecoos on 25-plates or newer, and those cars compete directly with heavily supported finance offers on brand-new models. Derren Martin, automotive expert at Cazana, said: "When consumers can drive away in a brand-new car on a heavily supported finance offer for roughly the same monthly payment as a near-new model, it creates a genuine dilemma for used-car pricing." He added that how these volumes are absorbed will dictate future residual values across the whole market, legacy and challenger brands alike.

The 0.5% fall in 25-plate values is modest, Martin said, but a critical indicator to watch. It arrives as the wider used market holds firm: [Autotrader's October Retail Price Index](https://plc.autotrader.co.uk/news-views/press-releases/used-car-retail-prices-hold-steady-month-on-month-as-market-continues-to-show-resilience) put the average used car at £17,321, flat month on month and down 1.8% year on year, with cars selling in an average of 29 days, while used electric cars rose 2.8% year on year to £25,263 for a sixth consecutive month of annual growth. Electric demand grew 38.1% year on year against an 11.5% rise in supply, and EVs sold in 25 days on average, the fastest of any fuel type.

## The supply pipeline behind the stock

The used numbers are the lagged echo of new registrations. The UK market as a whole rose 5.4% in October to 152,730 cars and 9.8% for the year to date at 1,892,000, according to [the SMMT's October release](https://www.smmt.co.uk/year-end-push-lifts-ev-sales-but-mandate-gap-set-to-widen), with the Jaecoo 7 the month's best-seller on 3,412 registrations. Chinese brands took 21.6% of October registrations and 17.9% of the year to date, and those cars begin reaching forecourts as used stock within months through part-exchange and fleet returns. [DfT's vehicle licensing statistics for 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/vehicle-licensing-statistics-2025/vehicle-licensing-statistics-united-kingdom-2025), published on 29 April 2026, counted 42.3 million licensed vehicles in the UK at the end of 2025, so even a small share of a single year's Chinese-brand registrations adds up quickly against a fixed pool of buyers. The government's fleet is not immune to the same math: 2,012,000 licensed zero emission vehicles made up 4.8% of all licensed vehicles at the end of 2025, and those early EVs are the ones now setting used-electric price benchmarks.

## What it means for buyers and sellers

For buyers, the record stock is choice: a growing pool of nearly new Chinese-brand cars at prices that new-car finance campaigns are now undercutting. For sellers and dealers, the signal is that residual values on the newest Chinese plates are the first to move, which is exactly the pattern [our look at the third-quarter used market](/used-car-market-q3-2026/) flagged when supply began rebuilding, and the same pre-registration pressure [cap hpi warned about in late October](/dealer-auction-october-2026-afv-margins/). The arrival story behind these brands is one [we have covered from the start](/chinese-brands-uk-arrival/), and [September's record for them](/chinese-brands-record-share/) showed the same marques taking almost a quarter of new registrations that month.
