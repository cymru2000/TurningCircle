The Office for National Statistics published its October inflation figures at 7am today, and two motoring costs lead the upward charge. The Consumer Prices Index rose 3.6% in the 12 months to October 2026, up from 3.5% in September and the highest rate since September 2025, with transport, particularly motor fuels, the largest single contributor ([ONS, 18 November 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/october2026)). Within the same release, the transport insurance index reached 196.5, against 181.9 in October 2025, an annual rise of 8% ([ONS CPI time series D7F3, released 18 November 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/timeseries/d7f3/mm23)).

For a household renewing car cover this winter, the insurance figure is the one that bites: it is more than twice the headline rate and the fastest annual rise in the index since June 2024. At the pump, the average diesel price reached 199.5p a litre in October, the highest since ONS records began in January 1996, and motor fuel prices overall rose 33.2% over the year ([ONS, 18 November 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/october2026)).

## The diesel record, in context

The ONS average is measured across all UK forecourts, not at premium outliers, which is what makes 199.5p significant. Filling a 55-litre family tank at that rate costs about £110. Britain crossed £2 a litre on average for the first time on [2 October](/diesel-hits-two-pounds/), and retailers have been trying to hold the line since: [Asda capped diesel at 189.7p across its Superstore forecourts on 14 November](/asda-fuel-price-cap-159p-diesel-189p/). The national average now sits just under the record even so, and the supply disruption behind the surge is still running, which is the risk sitting on top of the price already at the pump.

## Why insurers are paying out more

The insurance rise tracks what companies pay in claims rather than a pricing decision taken in isolation. The Association of British Insurers said motor insurers paid £3.2 billion in claims in the third quarter of 2026, matching the record set in the second quarter and 8% higher than a year earlier, with the average claim at £4,900, up 1% ([ABI premium tracker, reported 10 November 2026](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/news/motor-insurance-payouts-remain-at-record-32bn/1460222.article)). The average windscreen claim reached £298, up 9% year on year. Vehicle maintenance and repair prices rose 5.2% in the year to October, while spare parts and accessories eased to 2.8% from 5.1% a month earlier ([Insurance Business on the ONS figures, 18 November 2026](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/auto-motor/car-insurance-inflation-hits-8-as-uk-cpi-rises-for-a-fourth-month-597893.aspx)).

Chris Bose, the ABI's director of general insurance and international policy, said: "While competitive premiums are welcome news for customers, persistently high claims costs are putting significant pressure on insurers." He pointed to the Government's Motor Insurance Taskforce as the route to bringing costs down.

## The forecast already assumes bigger rises

EY's analysis of the motor market, published in July, expects insurers to pay out around £1.08 in claims and expenses for every £1 of premium earned in 2026, a net combined ratio of 108%, and forecasts premium rises of 4% this year and a further 12% in 2027, 16% over the two years ([EY, 23 July 2026](https://www.ey.com/en_uk/newsroom/2026/07/ey-latest-motor-insurance-results-analysis)). Dan Beard, UK insurance partner at EY, described "a difficult combination of lower earned premium income and persistent cost inflation", and warned that Middle East tensions could worsen the ratio by a further five to ten percentage points. Those are forecasts rather than prices already set, but they are the clearest signal yet that the cheap-renewal era of 2024 and 2025 is over. The same squeeze shows in household budgets elsewhere: [our coverage of the Bank Rate rise to 4% on 5 November](/bank-rate-4-percent-car-finance/) sets out what that means for car finance.

## Quoted prices versus prices paid

Two measures are now moving in the same direction but by different amounts, and the difference is worth understanding before renewal season. Defaqto's index of competitive quoted premiums ended September 2026 2.7% higher than a year earlier, the first annual rise since 2023, though the third quarter added just 0.5% ([Defaqto Motor Insurance Price Index, released 8 October 2026, via Insurance Times](https://insurancetimes.co.uk/news/motor-premiums-see-first-annual-rise-since-2023/1459899.article)). Stephen Kennedy, director at Defaqto, called it "an important change in the annual picture following two years of sustained price reductions".

The ABI's measure of the average premium actually paid slipped £4 in the third quarter to £562, still 2% above a year earlier but below inflation. The two series measure different things: Defaqto tracks quotes for identical drivers, while the ABI averages what customers really paid, which picks up shoppers switching to cheaper cover or taking higher excesses. The ONS transport insurance index sits closer to the renewal experience because it follows broadly comparable cover over time; it rose 1.3% in October alone, from 193.9 to 196.5 ([ONS time series D7F3, 18 November 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/timeseries/d7f3/mm23)).

## What it means at renewal

With claims costs climbing and quoted prices turning up, the gap between the cheapest and dearest quote for the same driver matters more than the average rise does, so comparing five quotes rather than accepting the first renewal pays. A higher voluntary excess trims the premium, but repair inflation makes each claim more expensive, so that trade-off is sharper than it was two years ago. And the 8% rise applies across fuel types: for anyone weighing an electric car, [insuring an EV still costs more than a petrol equivalent](/ev-insurance-costs/), even as the rest of the running-cost gap closes, so insurance is not a reason to switch on its own this winter.
