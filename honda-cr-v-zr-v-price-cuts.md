Honda cut the list price of both its UK SUVs on 2 November, taking £4,080 off the entry CR-V and £1,310 off the entry ZR-V. The CR-V e:HEV now starts at £43,995, down from £48,075, while the smaller ZR-V opens at £35,215 rather than £36,525. [Auto Express carried the new prices](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/honda/cr-v/370808/honda-cr-v-prices-slashed-thousands-zr-v-gets-cheaper-too) on the day, and [Fleet News put the reduction at up to £4,080](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/honda-cuts-pricing-for-cr-v-and-zr-v-suvs).

The biggest number is not the most consequential one. The top ZR-V, the Advance, now lists at £39,585. That leaves it £415 below the point at which a petrol or hybrid car becomes liable for the expensive car supplement, and crossing back under that line changes the tax bill for five years.

## What the range costs now

The CR-V e:HEV comes in two grades. Elegance is £43,995 and Advance is £46,995. The plug-in CR-V, badged e:PHEV, starts at £44,995 in Elegance Tech trim and reaches £47,995 for Advance Tech, so a buyer who wants the plug pays £1,000 more than the entry hybrid.

The ZR-V is simpler. Elegance is £35,215, Sport is £37,455, and Advance is £39,585. As a percentage the CR-V move is the deeper one, at 8.5 per cent on the entry car against 3.6 per cent for the ZR-V.

## Why Honda moved

Honda registered 17,143 cars in Britain in the first nine months of 2026, against 20,497 over the same period last year, a fall of 16.4 per cent, [according to SMMT's registration tables](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/car-registrations/). The market grew 10.2 per cent over the same stretch to 1,739,271 cars, so Honda's share slipped from 1.29 per cent to 0.98 per cent. September was weaker than the year as a whole: 4,149 registrations against 4,912 in September 2025.

Fleet News reports that 11 per cent of Honda's sales this year have been fully electric, and that for most of 2026 the brand had no electric car in its UK line-up. Its range now leans on the e:HEV hybrid system, with a plug-in option on the CR-V. That is a defensible place to stand while hybrids grow. It is a harder place to stand when the rest of the market keeps adding battery models.

Michael Doyle, head of automobile at Honda Motor Europe (UK), said: "This new pricing structure strengthens the appeal of both the CR-V and ZR-V, making our electrified SUVs an even more compelling choice for customers."

## The £40,000 line

The expensive car supplement adds £440 a year to the standard rate for cars with a list price above £40,000, and it runs for five years from the second time the car is taxed, [according to GOV.UK's rate tables](https://www.gov.uk/vehicle-tax-rate-tables). The same tables set the threshold at £50,000 for electric cars, a gap that opened on 1 April 2026 and that our [EV road tax guide](/ved-ev-owners-guide/) explains in full.

Two features of the rule give Honda's new prices their weight. The threshold is judged on the published list price before any discount, so a dealer reduction cannot pull a car back under the line. Options count as well, which is why Auto Express notes that the ZR-V Sport now has room for extras before it reaches £40,000.

The Advance had been above the line, so it carried the supplement. At £39,585 it is outside, provided the buyer adds no options. Across the five years the charge runs, that is £2,200 in tax avoided on a car that already costs less than it did last week.

The same rule works against the CR-V. Every version now starts above £40,000, from £43,995, so every CR-V buyer pays the supplement in years two to six of the car's life. Put a £47,995 electric SUV beside it and the electric car pays nothing, because its threshold is £50,000. That gap between the two thresholds does more work on this price list than any showroom discount.

The line catches a lot of buyers. Fifty-four per cent of new SUV stock on Autotrader is priced at £40,000 or above, as [our report on MG's affordability research](/mg-new-car-affordability/) set out last week, which makes a hybrid that starts below it unusual rather than typical.

## How it compares

Auto Express sets the CR-V against the Skoda Kodiaq, which starts at £39,390 with a mild-hybrid engine and £42,485 for its cheapest plug-in. Even after the cut, the CR-V e:HEV Elegance is £4,605 dearer than that Kodiaq entry point, and the CR-V plug-in is £2,510 above the Kodiaq's plug-in. The ZR-V, at £35,215, sits £4,215 above a Nissan Qashqai that starts at just over £31,000.

The comparison is not like for like on equipment, and Honda has priced its cars at the upper end of the family SUV class all year. What has changed is the distance. A £4,080 cut closes most of the gap on the Kodiaq without closing all of it.

## The market Honda is selling into

The hybrid segment Honda depends on is still growing. [SMMT's figures to the end of September](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/car-registrations/) put plug-in hybrids at 244,769 registrations, up 41.8 per cent and 14.07 per cent of the market. Self-charging hybrids reached 240,255, up 7.9 per cent, while petrol fell 3.8 per cent to 721,617. Battery electric cars were 454,947, up 30.2 per cent and 26.16 per cent of the market. Taken together, 939,971 new cars registered in the nine months were electrified in some form, or 54 per cent of the total.

Our [guide to hybrid sales in 2026](/hybrid-sales-2026/) looked at why plug-in hybrids have grown faster than any other powertrain this year. Honda's only plug-in is the CR-V, priced from £44,995, which puts it above the volume end of that growth. The cheaper ZR-V is a self-charging hybrid, and it competes in the part of the market where the price gap to a petrol car still decides most sales.

## What it means for a buyer

For anyone looking at a ZR-V Advance, the tax change is worth £2,200 over five years, and it arrives on top of a price cut. For a Sport buyer the useful part is the headroom under £40,000 for options. For a CR-V buyer the sticker is £4,080 lower on the entry car, and the supplement still applies to every trim, so the annual bill from year two is £640 rather than £200.

That is the practical way to read a price cut in 2026. The list price decides both what you pay and which tax band you sit in, and a manufacturer that wants to sell a well-equipped car has to think about the second number as carefully as the first.
