UK factories built 61,746 cars in October, up 6.2% on the same month last year and the third consecutive month of growth, according to [figures published today by the Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders](https://www.smmt.co.uk/car-production-up-again-but-road-to-recovery-fragile/). Including commercial vehicles, output rose 4.8% to 64,141 units.

The comparison is doing much of the work. October 2025 was exceptionally weak: a cyber incident halted production at one of Britain's largest vehicle manufacturers, and car output that month fell 25.0% to 58,117 units. Growth measured against that base flatters the recovery, which is exactly the point the SMMT makes in its own release.

## Exports are carrying the month

Cars built for export rose 14.2% to 51,002 units and accounted for 82.6% of car production, the highest monthly proportion since December 2024, when exports reached 84.5% of output. The European Union remained the largest destination with 53.7% of shipments, although volumes to the bloc fell 7.1%. Shipments to the United States rose 70.9% to a 17.3% share, and exports to Turkiye grew 57.7%. Japan and China completed the top five, with volumes falling 1.5% and 43.4% respectively.

Home output, meaning cars built for the UK market, fell 20.1% to 10,744 units. The domestic car market is not where the October growth came from; it came from overseas orders.

## Commercial vehicles are the weak half

Commercial vehicle production fell 22.9% to 2,395 units. Output for the UK market dropped 47.6% to 1,005 units, which outweighed a 17.0% rise in exports to 1,390 units. Year to date, CV output is down 45.0% to 23,250 units, a decline the SMMT has previously linked to structural changes in the sector, including the tax treatment of double-cab pickups.

Across the first ten months of 2026, UK factories built 628,678 cars and commercial vehicles, down 2.3% on the same period of 2025. Car production over that period was 605,428 units, up 0.7% year on year, so the cars half of the ledger is roughly flat while the vans half is collapsing.

## The same three demands from government

The SMMT used the release to repeat its three asks: substantive reform of the ZEV mandate, lower industrial energy costs, and protection of UK-EU trade. It warned that tougher rules of origin from 2027 could expose British manufacturers to a EUR 1.8 billion tariff bill in their biggest export market. Mike Hawes, the SMMT chief executive, said: "Three consecutive months of growth are welcome, but UK vehicle production remains under intense pressure. The industry has the ambition and capability to grow, but in a fiercely competitive global market, investment will go where conditions are right."

We covered the September rebound and the weak base it was measured against in [our piece on the September figures](/smmt-september-production/), and the tariff risk in [our look at the UK-EU summit outcome](/eu-uk-summit-rules-of-origin-tariff-warning/). The independent outlook cited with today's figures, produced by AutoAnalysis, projects light vehicle production will rise to 752,000 units in 2027 as new models enter production, up from a forecast 731,000 this year.

## What the October base really was

The 6.2% rise is measured against a month in which output across the sector slumped by a quarter because of the cyber incident, so the year-on-year number overstates the underlying momentum. The cleaner read is the run of three consecutive monthly increases, which is the first sustained growth the sector has posted this year, and the export share, which at 82.6% shows overseas demand rather than domestic buyers is setting the pace. That matters for the policy argument: the SMMT's case for cutting industrial energy costs rests on the fact that four in five cars built here leave the country, so a factory cost that only applies in Britain is a cost its competitors do not carry.

## Where the 2027 recovery is supposed to come from

The AutoAnalysis outlook published with the figures expects light vehicle output to reach 752,000 units in 2027, up from a forecast 731,000 for 2026, with the growth coming from new models entering production. Most of those models are electric, which ties the production story back to the ZEV mandate debate: the same release that reports the October rise repeats the industry's warning that mandate targets running ahead of demand are draining the money that would otherwise fund those programmes. The two halves of the SMMT's message, growth is possible and policy is the risk to it, are the same message.

## The domestic market is the soft spot

Home car output fell 20.1% in the month, and total home vehicle output fell 23.6% to 11,749 units. With new car registrations still growing nationally, the fall in home production says the UK plants are not supplying the UK market's growth; they are supplying export demand. For a buyer, that has a practical consequence: the cars being built in Britain in volume are increasingly the ones being shipped abroad, and the models aimed at UK forecourts are a smaller share of what the factories make. We looked at what that means for the factories themselves in [our piece on UK manufacturing stakes](/uk-manufacturing-stakes/).

## Reading the numbers without the headline

Two figures in the release are worth separating from the growth story. The 82.6% export share is a record for the post-2024 period but still below the 84.5% peak of December 2024, so "highest since December 2024" is accurate and "all-time high" is not. And the 2.3% year-to-date decline is entirely a commercial vehicle story: car production is up 0.7% over ten months while CV output is down 45.0%. A single headline that blends the two, "production up 4.8%", describes a month; the ten-month figure, down 2.3%, describes the year.
