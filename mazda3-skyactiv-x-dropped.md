The 2027 Mazda3 has been announced for the UK with a new trim level, a new grey paint option and a driver monitor fitted as standard. The change that matters is a deletion. The Skyactiv-X engine is no longer offered in the Mazda3, and the saloon bodyshell has gone with it.

Mazda's [announcement of the revised range](https://www.insidemazda.co.uk/2026/10/28/simplified-2027-mazda3-range-features-new-homura-plus-grade/) sets it out plainly. The car is now sold as a hatchback only, with the 140ps 2.5-litre e-Skyactiv G engine as the sole choice. The model that was going to show petrol still had a big idea left in it is now a one-engine, one-body hatchback arriving in showrooms before the end of the year.

## The engine that was meant to move petrol forward

Skyactiv-X was not a routine update. When Mazda put it into production in 2019, it was the [first commercial petrol engine to use compression ignition](https://dieselnet.com/news/2019/06mazda.php), a combustion process normally confined to diesels. Mazda calls the system SPCCI, for spark controlled compression ignition. A small spark-ignited charge raises the pressure inside the cylinder far enough for the rest of a lean mixture to ignite under compression, and the engine switches between that mode and ordinary spark ignition as conditions allow.

The first version ran a compression ratio of 16.3:1, close to a diesel, and Mazda quoted 180hp. A 2021 revision [lowered the ratio to 15.0:1 and lifted output to 186ps](https://mazdamediapacks.com/en/technology/press-releases/e-skyactiv-x-engine-technology.html), with 240Nm of torque at 4,000rpm. In lean mode the engine could run an air-fuel ratio of [up to 40:1](https://www.mazda.co.uk/why-mazda/skyactiv-x), against the 14.7:1 of a conventional petrol engine. At launch Mazda said the design would use [20 to 30 per cent less fuel](https://news.mazdausa.com/news-releases?asPDF=1&item=123190) than its own 2.0-litre Skyactiv-G.

All of that earned the engine attention and awards. What it did not do was sell in the volume that justifies a separate powertrain line. The Mazda3 and the CX-30 were the only two cars that carried it. Mazda has not given a reason for removing it from the hatchback. The decision to keep it in the CX-30 for the time being, [as Auto Express reports](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/mazda/qoros-3/370787/streamlined-mazda-3-range-drops-unusual-skyactiv-x-engine), shows where the remaining demand sits.

## What replaces it

The 2.5-litre e-Skyactiv G is a different kind of engine. It is larger, it produces 140ps rather than 186ps, and it burns fuel in an entirely conventional way, with cylinder deactivation when the load is light and a mild-hybrid system that recovers energy under deceleration.

The swap costs 46ps and gains 500cc. A larger capacity engine makes its torque lower down the rev range, which suits the automatic gearbox, and the mild-hybrid system covers the moments when the engine is working hardest from a standstill. It also brings the whole range onto one powertrain, which reduces the number of variants Mazda has to build and homologate for a car that sells in modest numbers.

The official figures Mazda publishes for the current Mazda3 are [45.6 to 48.7 mpg and 132 to 141g/km of CO2](https://www.mazda.co.uk/cars/mazda3-hatchback/) on the WLTP test. Those numbers cover the 2.5-litre range that the 2027 car continues.

## The numbers behind the decision

Mazda's own sales figures explain the shape of the range better than any statement. The company says it has sold [28,664 Mazda3s in the UK since May 2019](https://www.insidemazda.co.uk/2026/10/28/simplified-2027-mazda3-range-features-new-homura-plus-grade/), including the saloon, which works out at roughly 3,800 cars a year. Global production has passed 1.5 million, and Europe accounts for more than 180,000 of those. Britain is a small part of a large programme.

The market it competes in still runs on petrol. [SMMT figures for the first nine months of 2026](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/car-registrations/) show 721,617 petrol registrations and a 41.49 per cent share, down 3.8 per cent on the same period last year. SMMT counts petrol mild hybrids in that total, which is where the Mazda3's M Hybrid system puts it. Battery electric cars took 26.16 per cent over the same nine months, and plug-in hybrids 14.07 per cent.

The pressure at the bottom of the market is coming from new brands rather than established ones. [Geely reached 15,264 UK registrations in its first year](/geely-first-year-uk/) with a single model, and the Chinese groups entering Britain have been taking share in the segments where a Mazda3 would once have been an obvious choice. Simplifying the range of a slow-selling hatchback is one way to keep it on the price list without spending on a powertrain few buyers chose.

## What the range looks like now

Six grades remain: Prime-Line, Centre-Line, Homura, Exclusive-Line, Homura Plus and Takumi. The new Homura Plus sits above Homura and adds a 12-speaker Bose system, black leather trim, power-adjustable seats and a 360-degree camera. Prime-Line and Centre-Line come with a manual gearbox only, and the other four grades offer a choice of manual or automatic.

Mazda has made driver monitoring standard across the range, added a paint colour called Aero Grey, and revised the automatic emergency braking and lane keeping software. The saloon, which has been part of the line-up since this generation arrived, is gone from the UK.

Jeremy Thomson, managing director of Mazda Motors UK, said the car "brings subtle upgrades, plus a new Homura Plus model, and crucially with no price increase from the previous model year". Mazda has not published the full price list for the new grades. The outgoing range ran from [£25,010 to £34,810](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/mazda/3) according to Auto Express's review, which gives a rough idea of where the 2027 car will sit.

## What it means for buyers

Anyone choosing a Mazda3 as a company car will be choosing a petrol car with CO2 between 132 and 141g/km. That sits in the middle of the petrol bands and far above the zero-emission band, a gap that now runs all the way to 2030/31 in the [company car tax tables set at the Budget](/budget-2026-company-car-tax/). The Mazda3 has no plug-in hybrid or full hybrid option in the UK, so there is no lower-CO2 version of the car to choose instead.

For used buyers, the Skyactiv-X Mazda3 becomes a car whose engine is no longer sold in that bodyshell. The engine continues in the CX-30, so servicing and parts do not disappear with it. Petrol values fell 2.2 per cent in October at three years and 60,000 miles, [according to cap hpi's monthly read](/used-values-october-2026/), while electric cars rose slightly.

The next engine is already named. Mazda has said the Skyactiv-Z family will eventually replace both the X and the G, again according to Auto Express, which means the Mazda3's longer-term future depends on an engine that has not yet reached production.

## The read

The Mazda3 is a well-regarded car that British buyers have not bought in large numbers. The 2027 update does not try to change that with technology. It removes a costly engine from a low-volume model, cuts the number of cars Mazda has to make and holds the price, which is a reasonable response to a market where the growth is electric and much of the competition is new.

Skyactiv-X remains a genuine engineering achievement. It is now a footnote in a crossover. A clever engine has to earn its place on the price list, and in Britain the buyers who might have paid the premium did not.
