Volkswagen Group's operating result for the first nine months of 2026 was €71 million. In the same period last year it was €5.4 billion. The [98.7% fall](https://www.volkswagen-group.com/en/press-releases/volkswagen-group-nine-month-results-underline-urgent-need-for-action-20799) is the kind of number that reads like a verdict on a company, and there is real trouble inside it. The detail matters more than the headline, because the group's own account puts electric cars at the centre of its margin problem rather than the solution.

This is not a collapse in sales. Revenue was €233.4 billion, down 2.2%, and vehicle sales fell 10.6% to 5.88 million. The operating result carries €8.7 billion of special items, including a €6.4 billion non-cash impairment of Porsche goodwill, about €1 billion of impairment adjustments at fully consolidated Chinese businesses, and costs linked to the planned sale of the Osnabrück plant. Before those items, the group made €8.8 billion and an operating return on sales of 3.8%.

Arno Antlitz, the group's chief financial officer and chief operating officer, said that return was ["too low to continue investing strongly in our future"](https://www.volkswagen-group.com/en/press-releases/volkswagen-group-nine-month-results-underline-urgent-need-for-action-20799).

The third quarter was worse than the nine-month average. Volkswagen posted a €5.86 billion operating loss between July and September, against a €1.30 billion loss a year earlier, on revenue of €75.3 billion. Deliveries fell 14.3% to 1.885 million vehicles.

Cash flow went the other way. Automotive net cash flow for the nine months was €4.39 billion, up from €1.80 billion, helped by lower investment. Net liquidity was €32.98 billion at the end of September. A company can have a poor profit year and a strong cash year at once, particularly when the largest charges are accounting writedowns rather than money leaving the business.

## The gap between an electric car and a petrol one

The sentence that matters is about mix. ["The market success of our electric vehicles is putting additional pressure on our earnings, as we currently earn less from electric vehicles than from combustion-engine vehicles,"](https://www.volkswagen-group.com/en/press-releases/volkswagen-group-nine-month-results-underline-urgent-need-for-action-20799) Antlitz said.

Buyers are not rejecting the cars. European order intake rose 4% to 3.0 million vehicles in the nine months, battery electric orders rose 39%, and electric cars made up around 40% of an order bank of 1.1 million. The Electric Urban Car Family, the group's small EV range, has taken around 150,000 orders.

The pressure is in the profit per car. The [Core brand group](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/volkswagen-group-profits-plunge-99-pecent), which covers Volkswagen, Skoda, Seat and Cupra, made €4.33 billion, down from €4.72 billion, and its margin slipped from 4.4% to 4.1%. The group's explanation names the cause: "Volumes and earnings contributions from more profitable combustion-engine vehicles declined." It is selling more of the cars that earn less and fewer of the cars that earn more.

The [Progressive brand group](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/volkswagen-group-profits-plunge-99-pecent), which houses Audi, Bentley, Lamborghini and Ducati, had a harder time. Its operating result fell 26% to €1.15 billion and its margin fell to 2.7%, with weaker volumes in China and North America and negative residual value effects. Porsche was the exception, with €1.49 billion of operating result and a 6.7% margin, against a loss a year earlier.

China is the other drag. The group's share of operating results from its equity-accounted Chinese companies was minus €113 million, against plus €744 million a year earlier.

## What it means in Britain

Volkswagen Group is not a distant German problem for UK buyers. Its six main brands accounted for 358,173 of the 1,739,271 new cars registered in Britain in the first nine months of 2026, 20.6% of the market, according to [SMMT data](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/car-registrations/). The same six brands registered 354,671 cars in the same period last year, when the market was smaller. Their combined volume grew 1.0% while the market grew 10.2%, so their share fell from 22.5% to 20.6%.

The brand-level picture is mixed. Volkswagen registrations were down 2.13% at 137,802 and Seat was down 28.41% at 13,362. Skoda grew 7.98% to 71,513, Audi grew 4.77% to 88,626, Cupra grew 6.07% to 32,632 and Porsche grew 4.42% to 14,238.

That matters because the response to the profit fall is a cost programme, and cost programmes change what gets built. Volkswagen has already confirmed plans to cut 100,000 jobs and halve its model range, as [Fleet News reported in September](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/volkswagen-group-to-slash-half-its-model-range). The nine-month figures show the workforce down 14,800 to 648,100, 2.2% lower than at the end of 2025.

The model range is the part British buyers will notice. A smaller line-up with fewer variants is easier to build profitably. It also leaves dealers with fewer cars to sell, and the group has not said which UK models will go, so any claim about specific cars would be guesswork.

## The plan and the risk

Volkswagen's full-year outlook is revenue of around €315 billion and an operating return on sales of up to 1%. Automotive net cash flow is forecast at €3 billion to €6 billion, with year-end net liquidity of €32 billion to €34 billion. The forecast assumes the current tariff situation does not change and does not include the effects of the group's 2030 restructuring or the sale of a majority stake in Everllence.

The €31 billion cost programme is meant to close the gap. It covers a streamlined model range, lower vehicle costs, simpler structures, more efficient plants and faster technology development. The results do not say when the group expects the EV margin gap to close, which is the number to watch over the next year.

There is no retreat from electric cars in the plan. The order bank is the evidence: 40% of it is electric, and the small EV family has taken around 150,000 orders. The task is to make those cars earn as much as the petrol and diesel models they replace. The political pressure to sell more of them is not going away, and [the fight over the 2030 ZEV target](https://turningcircle.co.uk/zev-2030-decision-haigh/) is still running.

Competitive pressure in Europe is part of the group's own explanation, and [newer Chinese brands](https://turningcircle.co.uk/geely-first-year-uk/) are part of that pressure. Volkswagen's answer is scale and cost, which is why the job cuts and the shrinking model range matter more than the €71 million headline.
