Bentley pulled the cover off the Torcal in London, and in doing so ended 107 years of combustion-only history. The car is Bentley's fourth model line and its first without an engine, built in Crewe and aimed at a broader group of buyers than the brand has chased before. The specifications are striking, but the more interesting story is the bet underneath them, because the top of the car market is now the place where electric demand is genuinely being tested, and the most expensive brands do not agree on the answer.

## What the Torcal is

It is a five-metre luxury SUV, and Bentley's numbers are as follows. Two permanent magnet motors draw on a 113 kWh battery at 800 volts for up to 375 miles of range, and the car [produces up to 888 PS and 1,350 Nm of torque](https://www.bentleymedia.com/en/newsitem/1835-the-all-new-bentley-torcal-makes-global-debut), the highest torque figure Bentley has ever quoted. At 400 kW, around 100 miles of range can be added in seven minutes, and Bentley gives a 10 to 80 per cent charge time of just under 20 minutes.

Two versions go on sale together. [The Independent reports the standard car at £173,300 and the sportier Torcal S at £200,000](https://www.independent.co.uk/cars/electric-vehicles/bentley-torcal-revealed-b3054614.html), with first deliveries expected in the second quarter of 2027. On the same outlet's figures, the standard car makes 810bhp and reaches 62mph in 3.4 seconds, while the S lifts that to 876bhp and 2.9 seconds.

Underneath, Bentley has fitted its first fully active suspension, which does away with anti-roll bars, and all-wheel steering that brings the turning circle down to 11.1 metres. The battery carries a ten-year, 100,000-mile warranty. Bentley has also kept the sensory side of the brand alive with a propulsion soundtrack built from recordings of drums, a viola and a bass guitar, shaped around the rhythm of its 6¾-litre V8 and tuned to respond to accelerator demand and speed.

## The market it is entering is split down the middle

What makes the Torcal interesting is not the car but the company it keeps. [As Reuters set out](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/bentley-tests-wealthy-buyers-interest-going-electric-with-first-ev-2026-09-23/), beyond Ferrari, Rolls-Royce is the only other major ultra-luxury manufacturer currently selling an electric car, so Bentley is joining a list that runs to two names so far. Ferrari arrived first with the Luce, a car that starts at about 550,000 euros, and it drew criticism from some enthusiasts for looking nothing like the brand's traditional sports cars.

The others have stepped back. Lamborghini, which shares a parent with Bentley in the Volkswagen Group, has delayed its first electric model to the end of the decade, and McLaren said in September that it has no plans for one at all. Both cited limited customer demand. Bentley itself previously pushed its first electric model back to 2026 and dropped its target of becoming fully electric by 2030, saying customers were not ready to make the switch.

That is the contradiction the Torcal sits inside. The loudest industry argument against the UK's zero emission vehicle targets has been that buyers, particularly at the premium end, are not moving fast enough, a debate we covered in our [look at the mandate review](/zev-mandate-review-halfway/). Bentley has now decided it can sell a six-figure electric car anyway, and has put money behind the decision: [Reuters reports a £350 million investment](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/bentley-tests-wealthy-buyers-interest-going-electric-with-first-ev-2026-09-23/) in the Crewe plant to build it, with UK suppliers across the project.

## The pricing is the strategy

The detail that tells you most is the price. A Torcal at £173,300 sits well below Ferrari's Luce and below the rest of Bentley's own range, and Frank-Steffen Walliser, the chairman and chief executive, described it to Reuters as "a more affordable Bentley ... that definitely opens new markets". He also made the design intent clear: "Our intention was always to make a Bentley first. We feel people are not asking for a completely different car just because it's electric."

The early interest appears to be coming from outside the existing customer base. Martin Page, the Torcal's product line director, told [The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/cars/electric-vehicles/bentley-torcal-everything-to-know-b3054621.html) that "40-odd per cent of those people are new to Bentley; they're people that have never owned a Bentley", and that the brand expects a slightly younger and more female-skewed group than its traditional buyer.

Those are expressions of intent from the company rather than order figures. The first honest test arrives with the first UK deliveries next year.

## Why it matters beyond Crewe

Luxury launches tend to be read as a signal about the wider market, and this one carries two. The first is about prestige and price. If a brand known for hand-built twelve-cylinder grand tourers can sell an electric SUV at the entry point of its range, the argument that electric cars cannot carry a premium reputation weakens, and that argument has been doing a lot of work in boardrooms for the past two years.

The second is about competition. Bentley's cheapest model is not only competing with Ferrari and Rolls-Royce. It is arriving into a market where [new brands from China](/chinese-brands-uk-arrival/) are pushing upwards with large, heavily equipped electric cars at prices that undercut the established luxury names, a shift we have tracked in our piece on that arrival. Bentley is responding by moving to the bottom of its own range rather than the top of the market, which is a notable change of direction for the marque.

For anyone shopping further down the price scale, the relevance is indirect but real. Engineering developed for expensive cars tends to filter downwards over time, and the selling points Bentley is pushing here, quicker charging and a longer range between stops, are the same ones doing the work in the mainstream market we tracked in our [guide to the cars arriving this quarter](/new-evs-final-quarter-2026/). The luxury end is not where the volume is, but it is where the technology's reputation gets set.

## The honest read

The Torcal removes the last easy excuse for the ultra-luxury sector. An electric car in this class can now match a combustion one on range, and charge in about the time it takes to drink a coffee. Neither of those things asks the buyer to give up any of the craft the brand is known for. Whether customers follow is a different question, and one that Bentley is answering with a product rather than a forecast.

The brand's own framing is the sensible one to keep hold of. The Torcal adds a powertrain to the range rather than replacing a Bentley with another Bentley, and the buyers who want a petrol Bentayga or a plug-in hybrid Continental can still have one. This is a test of appetite at the top of the market, and the results will show up in delivery figures, not in launch-week headlines.
