Britain's van market grew again in October. The pickup market did not. Light commercial vehicle registrations rose 11.4% to 25,510, the seventh consecutive month of growth, while pickups fell 17.7% to 1,764 and are down 52.9% for the year at 14,936. The Ford Ranger accounts for just over half of the pickups registered so far in 2026.

## The van market's seventh month of growth

The [SMMT's October release](https://www.smmt.co.uk/best-ever-october-for-new-electric-vans-yet-still-just-half-mandated-target/), published this morning, puts the light commercial vehicle market at 25,510 registrations, 11.4% ahead of October 2025. The year to date is 276,885, up 4.8%.

The volume is in large vans. The 2.5 to 3.5 tonne models that carry most delivery work rose 12.9% to 18,560 and took 72.8% of the market. Medium vans added 14.3% to 3,825. Small vans, the smallest category in the release, fell 11.7% to 462.

That matters to the pickup story because it rules out the simplest explanation. Businesses are still buying commercial vehicles. They are not buying this kind of commercial vehicle.

## Pickups are down to 5.4% of the market

Pickup registrations fell to 1,764 in October from 2,143 a year earlier, a drop of 17.7%. For the year they stand at 14,936 against 31,731, a fall of 52.9%.

The share tells the same story in a different unit. Pickups took 12.0% of all light commercial vehicle registrations in the first ten months of 2025 and take 5.4% now. In October alone, the share fell from 9.4% to 6.9%. A year ago one in eight LCV registrations was a pickup, and now it is one in nineteen.

The year-to-date figure is the one to trust, because it removes the month-to-month noise of plate changes and delivery timing.

## What the tax change did

From [6 April 2025](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim23151), HMRC stopped using the one tonne payload test to decide whether a double cab pickup is a car or a van for benefit in kind. The test had allowed a double cab with a payload of a tonne or more to be treated as a van, which meant the flat van benefit charge rather than a percentage of the vehicle's price. HMRC now assesses the vehicle as a whole, and most double cabs are as suited to carrying passengers as goods, so most are cars.

Capital allowances followed on [1 April 2025 for companies and 6 April 2025 for income tax](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/capital-allowances-manual/ca23511). A double cab treated as a car no longer qualifies for the annual investment allowance or full expensing, the reliefs that let a business write off a working vehicle against tax quickly.

Single cabs are unaffected. HMRC's manual still accepts that [a single cab with one row of seats is normally a van](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim23150).

The transition is worth understanding. A double cab purchased, leased or ordered before 6 April 2025 keeps the van treatment for that employer until the vehicle is sold, the lease ends, or 5 April 2029. The relief ends when the vehicle leaves that owner, so it is not a permanent property of the model.

The demand effect was immediate because the tax break had created a class of buyer. Ken Brown, LCV valuations editor at Solera Cap HPI, wrote in [AM Online](https://am-online.com/features-landing/executive-view-the-impact-of-bik-rule-changes-for-4x4-double-cab-pick-ups) that the lifestyle double cab sector "has undergone a dramatic decline since the BIK tax changes", with many users seeking more tax efficient alternatives.

## The Ranger holds half of what remains

The Ford Ranger recorded 7,650 registrations in the first ten months, 51.2% of the pickup market. In October it took 794, or 45.0% of the month's total. It is ninth in the year-to-date LCV top ten, one place behind the commercial Land Rover Defender on 7,747, and the only pickup in the table.

The concentration has a practical consequence. The other 970 pickups registered in October were shared across every other model on sale, which leaves a buyer with fewer direct comparisons and a smaller pool of new stock to choose from.

## Why the 4x4 numbers are not a straight swap

Four wheel drive LCVs rose 104.3% in October to 899 and are up 70.4% for the year at 10,121. It is tempting to read that as pickup buyers switching. The arithmetic makes it hard to sustain. The category has added 4,181 registrations this year while pickups have lost 16,795, so the growth covers about a quarter of the fall.

The [SMMT attributed](https://www.smmt.co.uk/septembers-record-electric-van-uptake-still-less-than-half-mandated/) a 221.3% rise in 4x4 registrations in September to bumper fleet orders, which is a different mechanism from households changing vehicle type. Cap HPI suggests some pickup users may have moved to high specification SUVs or cars, including electric and plug-in hybrid models with lower benefit in kind. The 4x4 line is part of the picture, not a replacement for the pickup market.

## What it means if you are buying

For a business, the decision is now a comparison between a car and a van, and a double cab sits on the car side. That means benefit in kind based on the vehicle's price and emissions, and no annual investment allowance or full expensing on the purchase. If the van treatment is the point, a single cab or a vehicle built around the load bed is the route that still works. The wider system is explained in [our guide to company car tax](/bik-company-car-ev-tax/).

For a private buyer, none of this applies directly, because benefit in kind is an employment tax. What applies is supply. Brown expects the vehicles registered before the change to create a short-term oversupply that presses used values, then, as fewer new pickups enter the fleet, a tighter used supply that could support prices later. That is a forecast rather than a price you can bank today.

The choice is narrower too. The Ranger takes half the volume and the rest is spread thin. Foton has confirmed a diesel mild hybrid double cab for Britain in early 2027, [a launch we covered in October](/foton-tunland-uk/), so at least one new entrant sees a future in the segment. It will arrive in a market half the size of last year's.

If the tax treatment was the reason for the double cab, that case has gone. If the load bed and the towing capacity were the reason, the vehicle still does the job. The tax bill is now part of what that job costs.
