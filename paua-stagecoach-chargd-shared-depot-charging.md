Commercial fleets running electric vans and trucks can now book charging at selected Stagecoach bus depots, after the Paua platform opened access to 13 Stagecoach Chargd locations offering 652 EV charging connectors to approved business fleets. The sites span the UK from Dover to Aberdeen, with locations in Cambridge, Oxford, London, Sunderland and Inverness, and give operators a new place to charge alongside the public network, home charging and their own workplace depots.

The arrangement adds shared depot charging to Paua's fleet platform, which already aggregates public charging, home charging reimbursement and operators' own depot chargers into one account. Access is limited to approved commercial fleets and depends on spare capacity at each depot, so it is a planned option within a wider charging strategy rather than unrestricted public charging.

## What the 13 sites offer

Chargd is Stagecoach's commercial EV charging proposition, set up to open spare capacity at bus depots to other fleets. The depots were originally equipped with high-powered chargers to support Stagecoach's own transition to electric buses, and that capacity is now available to approved business fleets through Paua when it is spare.

Mike Smith, business development director at Stagecoach Chargd, said: "Making our charging infrastructure available to other commercial fleets is a key part of the Chargd proposition. By partnering with Paua, we're giving fleet operators a simple way to access selected Stagecoach charging locations as part of their wider charging strategy." He added: "Shared charging infrastructure has a vital role to play in supporting the UK's transition to zero-emission transport, and we're pleased to be expanding access to our network through this partnership."

The 652 connectors across 13 sites are the first national-scale example of a bus operator's depot network being opened to other commercial fleets through a single platform, according to [Paua's announcement](https://www.paua.com/article/paua-stagecoach-chargd-shared-depot-ev-charging), published on 19 November.

## The demand behind shared depots

The Paua-Chargd deal lands as the fleet industry's appetite for shared infrastructure is well documented. Research by the Association of Fleet Professionals (AFP) found that [58% of van fleets would consider sharing charging infrastructure](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/paua-gives-electric-fleets-access-to-charging-at-stagecoach-depots) to make electrification more practical, and the AFP's own shared charging network, run with partner Evata, has reached ultra-rapid coverage in 15 of London's 33 boroughs in little more than a year, with potential to scale to around 11,000 sites nationally, according to the [AFP chairman's 2026 AGM address](https://www.theafp.co.uk/afp-2026-agm-chairmans-address/).

Niall Riddell, CEO and co-founder of Paua, said: "Fleet managers have been very clear about what they need. They need reliable access to charging in the places their vehicles actually operate." He added: "There is already an enormous amount of commercial charging infrastructure being built behind depot gates across the UK. Connecting fleets to infrastructure such as Stagecoach Chargd can help vehicles electrify faster, improve utilisation of existing assets and reduce the need to duplicate expensive infrastructure."

Riddell summed up the model directly: "Shared depots aren't a replacement for public, home or workplace charging. They are the fourth piece of the fleet charging puzzle."

## How it fits the wider charging picture

The public network the Stagecoach sites complement reached [126,140 chargers across 48,815 locations at the end of October 2026](https://www.zapmap.com/ev-stats/ev-charging-statistics), of which 30,060 were rapid or ultra-rapid, according to Zapmap's monthly statistics, which feed the Department for Transport's official figures. Growth is still positive but slowing relative to demand, which is why shared private capacity matters to fleets whose routes do not follow the public rapid network.

Government funding is also pointing at depot infrastructure. The second window of the [Depot Charging Scheme](https://find-government-grants.service.gov.uk/grants/depot-charging-scheme-1) opened on 11 November 2026 with £38 million for projects delivered in 2027/28, covering 60% of charge point and civil costs up to £1 million across all sites, and closes at midday on 26 February 2027. That window had already been delayed once from its planned 28 October opening, as [our earlier report on the missed date]( /depot-charging-window-misses-opening-date/ ) set out, so fleets that prepared bids for the original date gained extra time rather than losing the round.

For smaller operators the choice between charging options can be worth six figures. [Research for the Open Road Partnership]( /smes-150000-electric-van-cost-divide-cebr/ ) found a modelled 10-van firm with private charging saves £42,000 over five years, while one relying on public charging is £110,000 worse off, a gap of £150,000. Shared depot charging sits between those poles: cheaper and more predictable than the public rapid network, without the capital cost of installing a firm's own chargers. The same logic underpins the [driveway charger sharing campaign]( /charger-sharing-campaign-9-million-households/ ) aimed at the more than 9 million households without home charging, though that covers residential driveways rather than commercial depots.

## What fleet operators need to know

Access through Paua is for approved commercial fleets only, and capacity at each Chargd site is offered when it is available, not guaranteed at all hours. A route that depends on depot charging therefore needs a fallback, which is why Paua presents the Stagecoach sites as one of four channels in a single platform rather than a standalone answer. Operators of electric vans weighing over 3.5 tonnes can also count registrations towards manufacturers' ZEV mandate obligations under the Vehicle Emissions Trading Scheme, which changes the economics of the vans themselves, as [our look at the range gap between electric vans and cars]( /electric-van-vs-car-range-gap-vdg/ ) explains.

For Stagecoach, opening the depots monetises infrastructure that already exists. For fleets, it adds charging where vans actually operate, which is the gap the AFP says matters most.
