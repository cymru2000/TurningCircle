Sales of used petrol and diesel cars both fell to their lowest monthly levels of 2026 in October, according to MarketCheck data published this month. Used diesel led the decline, dropping 6.2% from September to [84,895 sales](https://www.marketcheck.com/uk/resources/press/used-petrol-and-diesel-sales-fall-to-lowest-levels-of-2026-as-october-sees-sharp-slowdown), while used petrol fell 2.5% to [182,198](https://am-online.com/news/used-petrol-and-diesel-car-sales-hit-2026-low-in-october). Both fuel types have now recorded three consecutive months of falling sales, and the figures point to fuel costs at the pump as the likeliest reason buyers are holding back.

October was also the first month of 2026 in which used diesel sales fell below 90,000, and used petrol sales dipped under the previous low for the year of 185,086, set in April.

## The October numbers

MarketCheck's monthly series shows the slide building through the autumn. Used diesel sales peaked at 103,288 in March, then fell to 98,731 in July, 93,476 in August, 90,495 in September and 84,895 in October. Across that July-to-October stretch, diesel volumes dropped 14.0%. Petrol followed a similar path: 198,483 in July, 196,832 in August, 186,775 in September and 182,198 in October, a fall of 8.2% since July. Combined, petrol and diesel sales went from 297,214 in July to 267,093 in October, a loss of 30,121 transactions in three months.

Alastair Campbell at MarketCheck said: "There are a number of factors that could be influencing buying decisions - with the rising cost of fuel likely to be the driving factor."

The weakness is not confined to one corner of the market. Both fuel types recorded their lowest monthly volumes of the year in the same month, which suggests a shared cause rather than a diesel-specific one.

## How October fits the wider used market

The monthly retail slowdown sits against a still-growing quarterly picture. In its Q3 figures, [published on 9 November](https://www.smmt.co.uk/used-car-market-records-second-quarter-of-growth-with-one-in-20-drivers-going-electric/), the SMMT put total used car transactions at 2,030,619, up 0.5% on the year and the best third quarter since 2021, with 6,056,169 transactions year to date, the strongest first three quarters since 2019. Petrol remained the dominant fuel with 1,148,576 sales, or 56.6% of the market, while diesel fell 5.3% to 623,354, a 30.7% share that the SMMT attributes to reduced supply coming through from the new car market.

Electrified alternatives gained ground over the same quarter. Battery electric sales rose 23.2% to 97,625, a 4.8% share of the market, and hybrid sales rose 25.9% to 135,204, or 6.7%. Non-electrified cars still made up 87.5% of transactions, down from 89.5% a year earlier. The Ford Fiesta topped the Q3 model chart with 73,500 sales.

Read together, the two datasets describe a market that grew over the quarter but cooled at the petrol and diesel end as the year closed. We covered the Q3 totals in [our quarterly report](/used-car-market-q3-2026/) and the growing hybrid supply in [the MarketCheck stock series](/used-hybrid-stock-100000/).

## Fuel prices are the pressure point

The timing lines up with the pump. The UK average diesel price hit [200.01p a litre on 2 October](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/uk-average-diesel-pump-price-hits-2-per-litre-first-time-2026-10-02/), the first time it has passed £2 in history. Prices have eased since, but the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero's [weekly road fuel release for the week commencing 23 November](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/announcements/road-fuel-prices-23-november-2026), published at 9.30am on 24 November, still shows elevated averages, and the RAC's live Fuel Watch averages on 24 November put unleaded at 160.30p a litre and diesel at 192.07p [across the UK](https://www.rac.co.uk/drive/advice/fuel-watch/).

The RAC's own analysis, [published on 17 November](https://media.rac.co.uk/petrol-should-fall-below-150-a-litre-as-wholesale-fuel-costs-plummet), said petrol had fallen 7.8p a litre and diesel 3.6p from their 5 October highs, but argued that wholesale savings were not being passed on quickly enough. Simon Williams, the RAC's head of policy, said: "If wholesale price reductions were passed on in full, the impact would be dramatic: petrol could fall to under 150p in the next few weeks and diesel to 190p." He also pointed out that average pump prices in Northern Ireland run more than 5p a litre lower than the rest of the UK, at 159.2p for petrol and 189.2p for diesel. We looked at the gap between oil prices and the pump in [our recent piece on the price lag](/brent-oil-fall-pump-price-lag/).

For diesel buyers in particular, the October record matters: even after the fall from 200.01p, diesel at around 192p a litre remains well above the level at the start of the year, and a 55-litre fill of diesel still costs roughly £12 more than a petrol fill at current averages.

## What it means for values

Falling demand is feeding through to trade prices. cap hpi's [November 2026 Car Market Overview](https://www.cap-hpi.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Car-Market-Overview-November26.pdf) puts values at the standard 3-year, 60,000-mile benchmark down 1.7% in the month, around £350, one of the weaker November movements on its series. Diesel recorded the steepest fall at that benchmark, down 2.6% or £590, and the largest fall of any fuel type at 10 years and 100,000 miles, down 4.5% or £210. Year to date, diesel values are down 11.7%, while battery electric values are down 4.7% and actually rose 0.6% in November, the only fuel type to rise.

That split reinforces the sales data: diesel cars are losing buyers and losing value at the same time, while the used EV segment that has driven growth this year continues to hold its price.

## What to watch

The next test is the SMMT's monthly transaction series for October and November, published on the SMMT's [used car sales data page](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/used-car-sales/), and MarketCheck's November retail figures in mid-December. If pump prices keep sliding toward the sub-150p level the RAC forecasts, some post-Christmas petrol and diesel demand could return. If they stall near current averages, expect the declines to extend into early 2027. Temporary relief is already on offer: Sainsbury's announced on 23 November an 8p-a-litre fuel discount for customers spending £60 or more in store between 25 November and 1 December, worth up to £8 on a 100-litre fill.
