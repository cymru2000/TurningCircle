No British car brand has ever done anything quite like this. Jaguar stopped selling new cars altogether, tore up its badge, its typefaces and its dealer network approach, and bet the entire company on a run of electric luxury models aimed well above where it used to play. The first of them, the Type 01, will be revealed in New York on 6 October 2026. It is not a new model in a range. It is the whole argument for the rebrand, compressed into one car.

## The journey so far

The plan has been public since the Type 00 concept appeared in December 2024, a two-door show car with paintwork in colours most buyers had never seen on a saloon. The production car keeps the naming logic: the 0 stands for zero tailpipe emissions, the 1 for the first Jaguar of a new era. What is coming on 6 October is the production version of that concept, a four-seat electric grand tourer.

The road to the reveal has been long. The debut was originally slated for 2025, then moved to September 2026, then to October, and Jaguar has now confirmed on its own site that [all will be revealed in New York on 6 October 2026](https://www.jaguar.com/en-us/jdx/copy-nothing/inside-type-01/explore.html). That page also carried the first proper look at the interior in August, and it is the clearest sign yet of what the rebrand means in practice: a dramatic central spine running the full length of the cabin, four individual seats, a low GT driving position, and a ClearSight rear-view display set into the base of the windscreen. Jaguar's chief interior designer, Thomas Holden, described it as an architectural statement creating four individual spaces, in contrast to the horizontal interior design norm.

The car has also been out in public more than most pre-production prototypes. A camouflaged example ran demonstration laps at Monaco ahead of the Formula E race in May, and the prototype made a North American debut at Monterey Car Week in August, culminating at the Pebble Beach Concours on 16 August in a distinctive new wrap. JLR's chief commercial officer, Leonard Hoornik, has said the car will launch "in a very, very special way", which is marketing language, but in this case the marketing has so far matched the scale of the bet.

## What we actually know

Here is the confirmed list, and it is short. Four seats. Electric. Built on Jaguar's dedicated electric architecture, the JEA platform. A global market launch in the first half of 2027, which will end a drought of new Jaguar models stretching back to the F-Pace's retirement in December. Reveal date and city: New York, 6 October.

Beyond that, almost everything in circulation is speculation, and it is worth being blunt about that. The widely repeated claims of a tri-motor setup with more than 1,000bhp and a WLTP range over 700km trace back to briefing material around the investor day in June, not to homologated figures, and Jaguar has published no official power, range or charging specifications for the production car. The same goes for price: the expectation of six-figure positioning comes from the brand's stated move upmarket, not from a published list. We will not quote a range or a price as fact, because Jaguar has not. Anyone who tells you they know what the Type 01 costs is guessing.

What the company has said on the record is about positioning rather than specification. At the June investor day, JLR's chief executive PB Balaji said the kind of performance attributes Jaguar wants can be delivered only through an electric drivetrain, and JLR has reiterated its commitment to keeping Jaguar all-electric even as it adds hybrid options to other platforms elsewhere in the group.

## Why it matters

The commercial context is unforgiving. JLR announced thousands of job cuts in September 2026, and Jaguar has now spent the better part of two years without a single new model to sell, in a market where its luxury rivals never paused. Porsche has been refreshing the Taycan, Audi has the e-tron GT, and Ferrari has joined the electric ranks with the Luce. The Type 01 will not just be compared with those cars on the road. It will be judged against two years of accumulated scepticism about whether a heritage British brand can reinvent itself as something genuinely modern.

There is a UK industry angle too. Jaguar's relaunch is the biggest single statement of intent in British carmaking at the moment the sector is getting run of investment news, from Nissan's Sunderland commitment to McLaren's planned new plant, and our [JLR restructure coverage](/jlr-restructure-2026/) looked at the pressure the group is under while it retools. Bentley, the other British luxury marque going electric, took a gentler path, revealing the Torcal with the existing brand intact and a £350m investment in its Crewe line, which we covered in [our piece on Bentley's first EV](/bentley-torcal-first-ev/). Jaguar chose the opposite: change everything first, show the car later. That makes the Type 01 reveal the moment the two strategies can finally be compared on metal rather than in strategy documents.

## What success looks like

Not a sell-out first year, and not a positive swirl on social media. The honest test is narrower. First, does the production car keep enough of the concept's presence that people stop asking whether Jaguar has lost the plot, and start asking when they can order one. Second, do the eventual specifications land close enough to the Taycan and e-tron GT class that the price Jaguar eventually asks looks rational. Third, does the order book open with real momentum in the first half of 2027, because the whole rebrand has been running on anticipation, and anticipation has a shelf life.

The new quarter is stacked with launches at every price point, and most of them are fighting for mainstream buyers. The Type 01 is the opposite kind of bet: fewer sales, higher stakes, at the very top of the market. If it works, Jaguar has a future. If it does not, Britain's most famous car name becomes a case study. The covers come off in New York in a week.
