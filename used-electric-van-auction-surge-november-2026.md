The used van market is holding its strength into the end of 2026, with auction house Manheim reporting that sales volumes rose 5.2% compared with the first five months of the year, vans are selling in an average of 11.1 days, and guide price performance has improved to 99.1% ([Motor Trader, 25 November](https://www.motortrader.com/motor-trader-news/automotive-news/manheim-reports-rise-in-volumes-and-ev-demand-25-11-2026)). The standout movement is in electric vans, where the average number of sales rose 78.9% year on year, buyer participation more than doubled, and average selling prices climbed 11.3% to £9,812.

## The numbers behind the November report

Manheim Auction Services, part of Cox Automotive Europe, published its latest commercial vehicle figures on 25 November. Beyond the electric van surge, the average age of vans sold rose to 66.5 months and average mileage fell 11.5%, which the company reads as a sign that younger electric vehicles are entering the wholesale market in greater numbers. First-time conversion rates for van customers improved from 59.9% to 79.1%, meaning nearly four in five vans now sell without returning to the lanes. Average selling prices across all vans eased 6.0% to £8,572, which Manheim attributes to improved availability rather than weak demand. Guide price performance, at 99.1%, means vendors are realising almost the full published guide value on the stock they bring.

Matthew Davock, commercial vehicles director at Manheim UK, said the used van market "continues to perform exceptionally well", with volumes increasing and vehicles selling quickly despite wider pressures on vehicle pricing and operating costs. On electric vans he added that confidence in electric commercial vehicles is "clearly strengthening as they become a more established part of the used market" ([Motor Trader, 25 November](https://www.motortrader.com/motor-trader-news/automotive-news/manheim-reports-rise-in-volumes-and-ev-demand-25-11-2026)).

## Why electric vans are catching on at auction

The auction data lines up with the new market. The new light commercial vehicle market grew 11.4% in October to 25,510 registrations, its seventh consecutive month of growth, and battery electric vans set a record October with 3,369 registrations, up 58.0% and a 12.9% share ([SMMT, 5 November](https://www.smmt.co.uk/best-ever-october-for-new-electric-vans-yet-still-just-half-mandated-target/); [SMMT LCV data page](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/lcv-registrations/)). Year to date, 30,728 electric LCVs have been registered, up 26.7%, for an 11.0% share. SMMT's October outlook puts the full-year LCV market at 329,000 units, up 4.2%, with electric vans to 3.5 tonnes reaching a 10.4% share for the year ([SMMT market outlook, October 2026](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/uk-new-car-and-van-outlook/)). As those vans reach three years old they are feeding the wholesale lanes, and auction buyers, many of them self-employed tradespeople, are absorbing them.

The same pattern is visible in residual values. Solera cap hpi's latest LCV report put used electric small van values up 3.1% at the three-year, 60,000-mile benchmark, against a 2.2% fall for diesel vans, as we covered in [our piece on used electric van values](/used-electric-van-values-rise/). JD Power (Glass's) analysis for Fleet News found that sales of used battery-electric LCVs at auction grew by nearly 132% over the last 12 months, with small electric vans making up 59.1% of those sales in October and large electric vans achieving the highest average price at over £11,950 ([Fleet News, 20 November](https://fleetnews.co.uk/news/opinion-latest-new-and-used-lcv-market-update-analysis)). Battery state-of-health reports in auction catalogues are part of the reason buyers feel confident bidding, and average electric van mileage at auction fell by nearly 1,400 miles to 23,656 in the same analysis, pushing the average sale price up 2.2% to nearly £8,150.

## The supply side is still thin

For all the growth, used electric vans remain a small slice of the wholesale market. In October, battery electric LCVs accounted for just 4.5% of vans on sale at auction, down 0.9 points on the month, against 90.4% diesel ([Fleet News, 20 November](https://fleetnews.co.uk/news/opinion-latest-new-and-used-lcv-market-update-analysis)). Manheim's own earlier reporting shows how far the market has come and how far it has to go: in Q2 2026, while overall van volumes fell 6.5%, electric van volumes rose 6% and first-time conversion for electric vans climbed from 69.6% to 82.2%, with average electric van prices holding above £9,500 ([Fleet News on Manheim's Q2 report, 23 July](https://fleetnews.co.uk/news/manheim-and-bca-report-steady-used-van-market)). The direction is consistent across every dataset: volumes up, conversion up, prices up, and stock still scarce enough that well-presented electric vans clear quickly. Diesel still dominates the lanes, but the gap is narrowing as new electric van registrations compound year on year.

## What it means for buyers and sellers

For sellers, the combination of 11.1-day average selling times, 79.1% first-time conversion and electric van prices up 11.3% makes a strong case for bringing stock to auction rather than trading it in. For buyers, especially small operators for whom fuel costs come straight out of their own pocket, a used electric van at £9,812 average is increasingly competitive with diesel, particularly with diesel averaging 193.29p a litre in the week commencing 23 November ([DESNZ weekly road fuel prices](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/weekly-road-fuel-prices)). The wider context is that the new electric van market, while still less than half the 24% mandate target, is growing fast enough that used supply will keep rising, which should keep auction prices competitive.
