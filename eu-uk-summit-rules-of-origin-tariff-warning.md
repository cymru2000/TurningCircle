Europe's car industry has issued a joint warning that new trade barriers threaten an €80 billion a year automotive relationship between the UK and the EU, and has called on leaders at Friday's EU-UK summit to resolve two disputes before tariffs begin on 1 January 2027. In a statement published on 17 November, the European Automobile Manufacturers' Association (ACEA) and the SMMT said the proposed EU Industrial Accelerator Act risks excluding UK-built vehicles, parts and batteries from "Made in Europe" incentives and procurement, while tougher rules of origin under the Trade and Cooperation Agreement come into force in six weeks.

Industry surveys cited by the two associations suggest fewer than one in four battery electric and plug-in hybrid cars and vans traded across the Channel next year will comply with the new rules, exposing hundreds of thousands of vehicles to a 10% tariff. The bill for 2027 alone is put at almost €400 million on UK exports to the EU and €1.4 billion on EU exports to the UK.

## The two deadlines closing in

From 1 January 2027, the final stage of the Trade and Cooperation Agreement's product-specific rules of origin applies to electrified vehicles. At least 55% of a vehicle's value must then originate in the UK or the EU, up from 45% this year, and the propulsion battery pack must itself be originating, with packs required to reach 70% originating content and cells 65%, against 60% and 50% under the current transitional rules, according to the [European Parliament's research service summary of the TCA thresholds](https://europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2024/757643/EPRS_ATA(2024)757643_EN.pdf). Vehicles that fail the test face the standard 10% tariff on their full customs value, in either direction.

The rules were originally due to tighten in January 2024, and the EU and UK delayed them once, to the end of 2026, under a December 2023 decision that included a lock-in mechanism intended to rule out further extensions before 2032. Even so, the UK car industry is preparing to press for a two-year delay, [the Guardian reported on 12 November](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/12/uk-car-industry-rules-of-origin-brexit-ev-tariffs-eu), after an initial European proposal suggested a shorter postponement.

## What the tariff bill looks like

The two associations put the combined 2027 exposure at almost €1.8 billion: roughly €400 million on UK-built vehicles exported to the EU and €1.4 billion on EU-built vehicles coming the other way, per the [joint ACEA-SMMT statement of 17 November](https://acea.auto/press-release/europes-automakers-urge-united-action-to-avert-punishing-trade-threats-ahead-of-eu-uk-summit/). ACEA's own modelling, set out in a [letter to EU leaders dated 16 September](https://www.acea.auto/files/20260916-ACEA-Letter-on-TCA-ROO.pdf), is more granular. It expects around 520,000 electric cars and vans worth €17.9 billion to be exported from the EU to the UK in 2027, and estimates that 82% of them, about 426,000 vehicles, would fail the new origin rules and attract the 10% duty, a cost of €1.47 billion in 2027 alone.

The SMMT's industry surveys point the other way and give the headline the associations are using: fewer than one in four battery electric and plug-in hybrid cars and vans traded across the Channel will comply. The SMMT's [matching release on its own site](https://www.smmt.co.uk/europes-auto-makers-demand-united-action-to-avert-punishing-trade-threats-ahead-of-eu-uk-summit/) carries the same €400 million and €1.4 billion split and adds that EU-built cars accounted for 61.6% of UK battery electric sales in 2025 and 49% of plug-in hybrids, which is why the €1.4 billion side lands on British buyers as much as on exporters.

Mike Hawes, the SMMT's chief executive, put the case in the statement: "Europe cannot build a stronger automotive industry by cutting the UK out of the markets that make it competitive." He added that pragmatic rules of origin are needed to keep electric vehicles tariff-free and affordable while battery supply chains localise.

## The factories on the front line

The exposure is structural, not marginal. Of the 717,371 cars built in the UK in 2025, 555,826, or 77.5%, were exported, and Europe received 56.7% of those shipments, ahead of the US at 15.0% and China at 6.3%, according to the [SMMT's full-year 2025 production release](https://www.smmt.co.uk/tough-year-for-auto-but-new-models-and-industrial-strategy-can-deliver-growth/) published on 29 January 2026. By volume, the EU takes 60.4% of all UK vehicle exports, according to the [SMMT's LSE Consulting report of 5 November](https://smmt.co.uk/eu-and-uk-must-deepen-partnership-or-lose-ground-in-global-automotive-race-new-lse-consulting-report-warns/).

ACEA's [EU-UK vehicle trade fact sheet](https://acea.auto/files/ACEA-fact-sheet_EU-UK-vehicle-trade_May-2026.pdf) gives the value side. The EU sent almost 1.5 million new vehicles to the UK in 2025 worth €42 billion, up 2.6% on 2024, making Britain the EU's largest new vehicle export market at 24.8% of the total by value. In the other direction, 362,998 new vehicles went from the UK to the EU in 2025, worth €10 billion, down 17% on 2024. The high-volume UK plants most exposed are Nissan's Sunderland works, which builds the Leaf, Qashqai and Juke, and BMW's Oxford plant, which we covered when production there was halted over parts shortages in [our look at the Mini stoppage](/mini-oxford-production-halt/). Oxford Economics analysis cited by ACEA puts UK automotive production's contribution to the EU economy at €24 billion a year and its support for EU supply-chain jobs at 250,000.

## Why both industries want the same fix

The second half of the joint statement concerns the Industrial Accelerator Act, the EU's draft "Made in Europe" law, which as proposed would tie public procurement and EV incentives to European content and could deny UK-built vehicles access to both. ACEA and SMMT want UK-built vehicles, components and batteries to receive equivalent treatment across all relevant provisions. The political shape of that ask was [set out in our October piece on the Made in Europe rules](/eu-made-in-europe/): France and Germany have proposed an opt-in for countries that were in the single market on 1 January 2020, which fits the UK, but conditioned on aligning with EU tariffs on Chinese-built cars. The LSE Consulting report the SMMT published on 5 November frames the stakes as 1.37 million people employed directly in vehicle manufacturing across Europe and Britain, with the EU taking 60.4% of UK vehicle exports while 30% of EU vehicle exports come to the UK.

## What Friday's summit can deliver

The second EU-UK summit takes place in Brussels on 20 November, with the prime minister meeting the European Council and Commission presidents, and a joint declaration is expected at the end, according to [pre-summit coverage of the summit agenda](https://eureports.com/2026/11/brexit-reset-summit-pairs-youth-residency-deal-with-fight-over-eu-car-rules/) published on 9 November. ACEA and SMMT want a strong joint statement resolving both the Made in Europe question and the rules of origin. Reporting ahead of the meeting, [electrive noted on 12 November](https://electrive.com/2026/11/12/eu-and-uk-could-delay-mutual-ev-tariffs-once-more/) that the European Commission is consulting the industry on shorter transition periods than ACEA asked for. ACEA's proposal, from its September letter, would keep the current battery-pack assembly rule until the end of 2029, require EU- or UK-made cells from 2030 and originating cathode material from 2032.

## What it means for UK buyers and sellers

For British buyers, the near-term risk is price. EU-built cars were 61.6% of UK battery electric sales in 2025, and if the €1.4 billion side of the tariff bill materialises, some of that cost could reach forecourts on European-built EVs and plug-in hybrids. For UK manufacturers, the mirror image applies: almost €400 million of tariff exposure on UK-built electrified exports, on a trade relationship the SMMT values at €80 billion a year. We looked at what is at stake in UK car making more broadly in [our piece on the manufacturing stakes](/uk-manufacturing-stakes/), and at the September production figures, where the EU took 57.5% of car exports, in [our look at the month's output](/smmt-september-production/).
