Half of all learner drivers who took a practical car test in Great Britain in October 2026 waited no more than 10.3 weeks between booking and taking the test, according to figures the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) published on 11 November 2026. That median wait is the lowest since the measure began in 2019, when it stood at 6.6 weeks in May of that year, and it has fallen for six months in a row from a peak of 30.4 weeks in June 2021. The separate measure of current appointment availability, the weeks until at least 10% of weekly slots remain free, fell to 19.2 weeks in October, the lowest since September 2024.

The improvement is real but uneven. Nine of the ten UK test centres with the longest median waits in October were in Scotland, led by Callander at 31.5 weeks, while Inverness recorded a median wait of just 3.9 weeks.

## The two numbers that confuse everyone

DVSA publishes two different waiting-time measures, and they answer different questions. The median wait of 10.3 weeks records how long people who actually took a test in October waited between their first booking and sitting it. The availability measure of 19.2 weeks records the first week in the 24-week booking window where at least 10% of slots are still available, so it describes what someone booking today can see, not what past candidates experienced. The National Audit Office criticised the older availability metric in its December 2025 report because it does not show how long individuals actually wait, which is why DVSA began publishing the median alongside it in June 2026. Both figures for October 2026 come from the [DVSA's driving test and theory test data tables](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/driving-test-and-theory-test-data-cars), last updated on 11 November 2026 with data to the end of October.

## A record month for tests, but waits are still long

October 2026 was the busiest month on record for car driving tests in Great Britain. The DVSA conducted 198,111 tests, 15,679 more than in October 2025 and the highest monthly total since the series began in January 2019. Of those, 100,584 learners passed, the second time more than 100,000 people have passed in a single month, and the pass rate has now stayed above 50% for 11 consecutive months. Over the last 12 months the agency conducted a record 2,177,511 tests, helped by examiner numbers at their highest since July 2017 and 33,091 test slots provided through overtime in October alone. As of October 2026, 592,010 standard tests were already booked for future dates.

Even so, more than a quarter of October's tests, 27.5%, were taken more than 21 weeks after booking, while 43.4% were taken within seven weeks. Roads Minister Justin Madders said waiting times were "still far from where they should be" but that six consecutive monthly falls showed the government's action was "making a real difference".

## Where the waits are longest

The national median of 10.3 weeks masks a wide spread between centres. In October 2026 the median wait was 13.6 weeks in Scotland, 10.3 weeks in England and 8.0 weeks in Wales; among English regions the North East was longest at 12.7 weeks and the South West shortest at 7.9 weeks. At centre level, the longest median waits in October were Callander at 31.5 weeks, Arbroath at 27.4, Mallaig at 27.3, Kelso at 26.9, Aberfeldy at 26.1, Campbeltown at 25.6, Peebles at 25.1, Haddington at 24.0 and Dunoon at 23.9, nine of the ten worst in the UK, according to [DVSA's centre-level table DRT122F](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/driving-test-and-theory-test-data-cars), with the Scotsman reporting the Scottish picture on [23 November 2026](https://www.scotsman.com/news/politics/scottish-learner-drivers-are-having-to-wait-up-to-31-weeks-to-sit-their-tests-9367466). Inverness, by contrast, recorded a median wait of 3.9 weeks.

The Scottish Government's critics point to the local pattern: SNP MSP Lloyd Melville said learner drivers in Arbroath faced "a wait time of over half a year" while Callander was "even worse". A UK Government spokesperson said DVSA conducted 12,434 more tests in Scotland in the year ending October 2026 than in the previous year, and that examiner numbers were at their highest in recent years.

## Why the backlog built up, and what happens next

The backlog began in the Covid pandemic, when DVSA could not provide an estimated 1.1 million tests, and the National Audit Office estimated in December 2025 that around 400,000 bookings in the system were from that backlog. Demand has also risen beyond the pandemic effect: theory test passes rose 43% between 2019-20 and 2024-25, and provisional licence applications from the ten countries whose licences are not valid in Great Britain after 12 months rose from 98,000 in 2018 to 339,000 in 2024, according to the [NAO's investigation into car driving test waiting times](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/investigation-into-car-driving-test-waiting-times.pdf), published 17 December 2025. The NAO also found the old waiting-time metric understated real waits and that around 31% of surveyed learners had used a third party to book a test, sometimes paying up to £500 against the standard weekday fee of £62.

The DVSA's [2026 to 2027 business plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dvsa-business-plan-2026-to-2027/driver-and-vehicle-standards-agency-business-plan-2026-to-2027), published on 16 November 2026, commits to providing up to 2.3 million car practical tests by March 2027 and to bringing the national average wait down to seven weeks or fewer by autumn 2027, a date that falls after the plan period. Our [coverage of that plan](/dvsa-business-plan-2026-27-mot-adas-ev/) sets out the targets in full. New test appointments are released at 6am every Monday, 24 weeks ahead, according to [GOV.UK's booking guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/driving-test-appointments-release-dates-and-finding-earlier-tests), which also confirms the 10.3-week October median. The next monthly update, covering November 2026, is due on 9 December 2026.
