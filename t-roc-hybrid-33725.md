Volkswagen will open UK order books on 12 November for the T-Roc Hybrid, the first full hybrid it has sold in Britain, priced from [£33,725](https://newspressuk.com/releases/122270). The car's WLTP economy ranges from 4.9 to 5.6 litres per 100km, or about 50 to 58mpg, against 49.6mpg for the entry mild hybrid it sits above.

The T-Roc is Volkswagen's third-biggest UK model, behind the Golf and the Tiguan, with 28,075 registrations in 2025, almost one in six of the brand's cars sold here, according to [Volkswagen's press pack](https://www.vwpress.co.uk/press-kits/1083). A full hybrid gives it an answer for buyers who cannot charge at home, without the cost and weight of a plug-in hybrid.

## Five versions, one powertrain

The range runs from the Match at £33,725 to the R-Line 170 at £40,500, according to [Volkswagen's UK price list](https://newspressuk.com/releases/122270). Every version uses the same 1.5-litre TSI evo2 petrol engine and hybrid system, with either 136PS or 170PS.

| Version | Power | Price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Match | 136PS | £33,725 |
| Style | 136PS | £37,890 |
| Style | 170PS | £39,980 |
| R-Line | 136PS | £38,510 |
| R-Line | 170PS | £40,500 |

The 136PS car reaches 62mph in 9.7 seconds, the 170PS version in 8.5 seconds, and both are limited to 112mph. Drive goes to the front wheels through a single-speed transmission. [Volkswagen quotes CO2](https://www.volkswagen-newsroom.com/en/the-new-golf-hybrid-the-new-t-roc-hybrid-20802/in-brief-20803) of 113 to 127g/km depending on trim and wheels.

Standard equipment includes 18-inch alloy wheels, a 12.9-inch touchscreen, an eight-inch digital instrument display, heated front seats and a heated steering wheel, adaptive cruise control and automatic emergency braking. Volkswagen says the car meets current ULEZ standards.

## How it works without a plug

The system uses two electric machines. One drives the front wheels. The other acts as a generator, driven by the petrol engine, to keep a small battery charged, and regenerative braking adds energy as well. There is no charging socket.

Below about 40mph the car runs on the electric motor as often as possible, with the engine off unless the driver asks for more. Above that speed the engine becomes the primary power source and the motor assists under acceleration. In between, the engine can run decoupled from the wheels to generate electricity, which Volkswagen calls series drive.

The set-up uses a single-speed transmission, where Toyota's full hybrids use a continuously variable transmission.

## The official economy overlaps the mild hybrid

Volkswagen's [WLTP figures](https://www.volkswagen-newsroom.com/en/the-new-golf-hybrid-the-new-t-roc-hybrid-20802/in-brief-20803) for the T-Roc Hybrid range from 4.9 to 5.6 litres per 100km, depending on power output, trim and wheels. That is roughly 58mpg at best and 50mpg at worst. The mild hybrid is quoted at 49.6 to 50.4mpg and 127 to 130g/km of CO2.

So the hybrid's best figure is about 16% ahead of the mild hybrid, and its worst is level with it. Volkswagen says the advantage is greatest in town, where the car spends more time on electric power and the engine spends more time switched off.

The price gap is about £1,230. At [170.71p a litre](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/weekly-road-fuel-prices), the UK average for the week commencing 9 November, 10,000 miles in the mild hybrid costs about £1,565 in petrol. The same distance at 57.7mpg costs about £1,345, a saving of roughly £220 a year. The premium takes about five and a half years to repay. At the least efficient hybrid figure, the payback runs beyond a decade. Buyers who do most of their driving in town will beat that calculation. High-mileage motorway drivers will not.

## A shrinking corner of the market

The T-Roc Hybrid arrives as conventional hybrids lose ground. [SMMT figures for October](https://www.smmt.co.uk/year-end-push-lifts-ev-sales-but-mandate-gap-set-to-widen/) show 18,287 hybrid registrations, down 5.0% on the year before and a 12.0% share, the second monthly decline. Plug-in hybrids rose 30.1% to 22,898 and a 15.0% share, while battery electric cars rose 32.7% to 48,858 and 32.0%.

Year to date, hybrids account for 13.7% of the market, plug-in hybrids 14.1% and battery electric cars 26.6%. The plug-in hybrid has moved ahead of the conventional hybrid in share, which was not the case a year ago.

## The mandate does not reward it

The government is expected to cut the 2030 zero-emission target from 80% this week, [according to the BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqkg5g2970epo), with reports pointing to a figure of about 60%. Even after that change, a full hybrid does not count as a zero-emission vehicle. The market is running at 26.6% electric year to date against a 33% target for 2026, and the T-Roc Hybrid does nothing to close that gap.

That is not the point of the car. The T-Roc Hybrid is aimed at buyers who cannot charge at home and do not want the cost or the cable of a plug-in hybrid, a group Volkswagen has served only with mild hybrids until now. The case for the car is commercial rather than regulatory, and it explains why Volkswagen put the system in one of its three biggest UK models rather than in a niche one.

## What it means for buyers

A full hybrid makes sense for drivers without a driveway or a reliable public charger. The car charges itself and meets ULEZ standards. Volkswagen says it returns better urban economy than the mild hybrid it sits above, and that company car drivers will pay less benefit-in-kind than in the equivalent eTSI because the CO2 figure is lower.

The tax advantage is modest, though. Electric cars sit in a 4% band this tax year, as our [company car tax guide](/bik-company-car-ev-tax/) sets out. [Business Motoring](https://businessmotoring.co.uk/volkswagen-prices-t-roc-hybrid-from-33725/) reports a benefit-in-kind rate of 31% for the T-Roc Hybrid. A driver choosing between the hybrid and an electric car on tax alone will not find the hybrid close.

The Match at £33,725 uses the same engine and hybrid system as the £40,500 R-Line, so the case for the higher trims rests on equipment and power rather than efficiency.

## What to watch

The Golf Hybrid uses the same system and will follow in the UK. The first deliveries and independent road tests will show whether the official 50 to 58mpg range holds in British conditions, and whether the car spends as much time on electric power as the test cycle assumes.

The bigger question is price. At £33,725 the T-Roc Hybrid is £1,230 above the mild hybrid. Volkswagen's argument is that the alternative for a buyer without a charger is a petrol car, not an electric one. The hybrid only wins that argument if the buyer does the kind of driving where the electric motor earns its keep.
