Suzuki has cut the list price of the e Vitara, its first mass-produced electric car, by £5,000. [Auto Express reported the permanent reduction](https://autoexpress.co.uk/suzuki/370562/suzuki-swift-among-uks-cheapest-cars-again-brand-slashes-prices-range-wide) on 1 October, taking the entry point to £24,999 from the £29,999 the car launched at last summer. The change replaces the £3,750 "Suzuki Granted" customer saving the brand had been funding itself, and [Suzuki's own offers page](https://cars.suzuki.co.uk/offers-finance/e-vitara-offers-and-finance/) now lists the e Vitara from £24,999, with the larger-battery 61kWh version from £27,999.

## Why Suzuki is paying, not the government

The [Electric Car Grant](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/discount-of-up-to-3750-on-electric-cars-set-to-slash-costs-for-thousands) gives up to £3,750 off a new electric car priced at or under £37,000, but it only applies to models on the government's approved list, banded by sustainability. [The e Vitara is not on it](https://cars.suzuki.co.uk/new-cars/e-vitara/), a point Suzuki makes in its own small print: the car "does not qualify for the UK government's EV grant scheme". Nothing comes from the state, so any discount has to come from Suzuki. The brand's first answer, when the car arrived, was the self-funded £3,750 "Suzuki Granted" saving; the new answer is to fold a bigger cut into the list price and retire the scheme.

That contrast is the story. Hyundai opened UK orders for the IONIQ 3 today at £18,495 after the full grant, as we [report separately](/ioniq-3-orders-open/), while Suzuki is funding a comparable move from its own pocket because the grant does not reach its car. Both routes end in a cheaper electric SUV, and only one of them runs through the taxpayer.

## What the e Vitara costs now

Suzuki's current offers put the e Vitara at £24,999, with the 61kWh Motion at £27,999 and the Ultra versions above that. The £5,000 reduction is measured against the £29,999 the car was priced at when UK prices were first confirmed, and it is a permanent change to the list price rather than a limited-time offer. Suzuki said the adjustment makes its pricing structure more transparent, as [Business Motoring reported](https://businessmotoring.co.uk/suzuki-drops-e-vitara-price-to-24999-in-place-of-suzuki-granted-offer/).

Finance is doing as much work as the sticker. [Suzuki is offering 0% APR over four years with no minimum deposit](https://cars.suzuki.co.uk/offers-finance/e-vitara-offers-and-finance/) on the e Vitara, valid from 1 October to 31 December 2026. In the brand's representative example the 49kWh Motion works out at £229 a month over 48 payments with a £4,858 deposit on an 8,000-mile annual limit, and Auto Express reports the same car at £330 a month with nothing down.

## Range and what you get

Range depends on the battery pack. The 49kWh car is rated at up to 213 miles on the WLTP test cycle, and the larger 61kWh version at [up to 264 miles at its best](https://autoexpress.co.uk/suzuki/370562/suzuki-swift-among-uks-cheapest-cars-again-brand-slashes-prices-range-wide), which is the WLTP maximum for the strongest trim rather than a figure every e Vitara will match. The car is a compact SUV with a sliding and reclining rear bench, a boot that grows to 562 litres with the seats folded, and a standard fit of blind spot monitoring, rear cross traffic alert, lane keeping assistance and dual sensor brake support. Suzuki also points to a service-activated warranty that can run to ten years.

The e Vitara was co-developed with Toyota and Daihatsu, and the Toyota-badged version, the Urban Cruiser, sells the same platform at a higher price: it starts from nearly £32,000, so the Suzuki is now the cheaper way into the car underneath.

The line-up runs from the 49kWh car to the 61kWh version, and the bigger pack is available with the dual-motor ALLGRIP-e four-wheel-drive system, which Suzuki has long treated as a point of difference. Suzuki quotes a 10 to 80% rapid charge in 45 minutes on a charger delivering at least 70kW, and an efficiency range of 14.9 to 16.6 kWh per 100km across the range. In the compact electric SUV class it lines up against the Kia EV3, the Skoda Elroq and the Renault 4, and Auto Express notes that even after the cut it remains dearer than the Renault and the Fiat Grande Panda Electric.

## A range-wide repricing

The EV is not an isolated move. Suzuki has repriced much of its line-up for the fourth quarter, with the Vitara Hybrid down £5,300 to £21,999 and the S-Cross Hybrid down £5,050 to £22,999, alongside a £500 finance deposit allowance on qualifying cars. The Swift supermini starts at £16,499 after a £3,500 reduction. Auto Express put the e Vitara's cut in that context, framing it as a brand-wide reset rather than a single model offer.

## What it means for buyers

For anyone shopping a compact electric SUV, the arithmetic has improved twice over in a week. The e Vitara's entry price is £5,000 lower than at launch, the finance rate is zero, and the car now sits against the IONIQ 3 at £18,495 after grant and the other models joining the eligible list, which our [autumn grants update](/grants-autumn-update/) tracks. The two mechanisms are running side by side, a government grant on an approved shelf and a maker's own money everywhere else, and between them they are pushing the affordable end of the electric market down from both directions at once. For a buyer, the direction is the only part that matters.
