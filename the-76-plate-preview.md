On 1 September the 76 plate arrives. From that morning until the end of February 2027, every new car registered in Britain will carry the two digits that tell other road users it was bought in the second half of 2026. Twice a year the plate change does this: it gives the market a focal point, gives dealers a hook for offers, and gives buyers a reason to time their purchase. March and September between them account for the bulk of annual registrations, and September is traditionally the second biggest month of the year.

This year's September carries a bit more weight than usual, so it is worth setting out what the data says before the month begins.

## The market arrives in better shape than last year

The run-up has been strong. July was the market's eighth consecutive month of growth, with [156,571 new cars registered, up 11.7% on July 2025](https://www.smmt.co.uk/huge-ev-boost-in-july-yet-mandate-gap-persists/), the best July performance since 2019. Through the first seven months of the year, registrations reached 1,294,499 units, up 9.5% on the same period last year. The market is recovering towards pre-pandemic levels rather than booming, but the direction has been consistently upward all year.

The strength sits almost entirely with electrified vehicles. Battery electric cars posted a record July volume of [43,106 units, up 44.5% year on year, for a 27.5% market share](https://www.smmt.co.uk/huge-ev-boost-in-july-yet-mandate-gap-persists/). Plug-in hybrids took another 14.9% of the market and hybrids 13.2%, which means well over half of everything registered in July was electrified in some form. Petrol's share has slipped to 40.1% and diesel to 4.2%.

## Why September matters more than July

July is a middling month in volume terms. September is not. The SMMT notes that the new number plate factor typically drives [around one in seven of the year's new car registrations](https://www.smmt.co.uk/september-new-car-market-delivers-record-number-of-evs/) into September alone, which is a remarkable concentration for a single month. That is why the industry reads September as the real test of demand in a way it does not read July or August.

For buyers, the practical effect is straightforward. Dealers and manufacturers concentrate their strongest offers around the plate change: deposit contributions, low-rate finance and outright discounts, all aimed at getting cars registered on the new plate. If you have been holding off on an order, the next few weeks are when your negotiating position is at its best, since the industry has every incentive to close deals before the month is out.

## The electric question hanging over the month

September will also be the biggest single test yet of where electric demand actually stands. The industry's own forecast, published alongside the July figures, expects [2.18 million new car registrations in 2026, with battery electric cars taking a 27.4% share](https://www.smmt.co.uk/huge-ev-boost-in-july-yet-mandate-gap-persists/) of them. That is an upward revision from the 26.8% guided in April, but it still sits well short of the 33% the ZEV mandate requires for the year. We have covered the mechanics of that shortfall in [the EV mandate gap](/the-ev-mandate-gap/) and the [mandate's flexibilities](/zev-mandate-flexibilities/).

For electric buyers, the incentives are unusually rich right now. The Electric Car Grant takes [up to £3,750 off eligible new models priced at or under £37,000](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/four-more-ev-models-qualify-for-3750-discount-under-electric-car-grant-as-government-pumps-an-extra-15-billion-into-the-switch-to-electric), with a lower tier of £1,500 above that line, and the eligible list has grown steadily since the scheme launched in July 2025. Layer manufacturer plate-change offers on top and the effective price of several popular EVs is lower than it has been at any point in their model cycles. Our [electric car grant eligible models list](/grant-eligible-cars-list/) covers which cars qualify.

The one caveat worth carrying into September is that much of the electric growth is being bought as well as earned. The SMMT itself attributes demand to a mix of model choice, heavy discounting and government incentives, and it has been explicit that mandate targets need to be grounded in the reality of demand. September's electric share will be read closely on exactly that point: a big number achieved on discounts tells a different story from the same number arriving unassisted.

## What we will be watching

Three things stand out when the September figures land in early October. The first is total volume, since a month carrying one in seven of the year's registrations will either confirm or undermine the recovery narrative the rest of the year has built. The second is the BEV share, since September 2025 delivered a then-record 23.3% share and 72,779 battery electric registrations; beating the share figure this time round would mark clear progress, and the volume comparison matters just as much given a larger overall market. The third is the split between fleet and private buyers, since fleet demand is shaped heavily by company car tax treatment while private share reflects what people choose with their own money. We covered that tax angle in [company car tax for EVs](/bik-company-car-ev-tax/).

If you are buying rather than spectating, the advice is simple. Order with enough lead time to secure a 76-plate registration, compare the plate-change offers against the grant-eligible electric models, and do not assume the September hype means you should skip the negotiation. Plate-change month is when the market most wants your business. Use that.
