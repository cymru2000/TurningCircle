Geely launched in Britain on 23 October 2025 with a single electric SUV. Twelve months later the [company's own review of the year](https://geelyautoukmedia.com/en/news-articles/one-year-of-geely-auto-in-the-uk-brand-celebrates-rapid-growth-and-growing-footprint.html) counts 15,264 registrations, 80 operational retail locations, 92 employees and a new headquarters at Ansty Business Park in Coventry.

Those are the numbers a new brand chooses to publish on its birthday, so they are worth reading as a claim as well as a result. They are also specific enough to compare with something, which most launch announcements are not.

## What a first year looks like

Fifteen thousand cars in a year is a modest number by the standards of an established brand and a serious one for a newcomer starting from no dealers, no service network and no recognition. Geely's September was its strongest month, with 5,155 registrations, and the company says it remains on track for 20,000 registrations in 2026, according to [coverage of the anniversary](https://ev.com/news/geely-auto-uk-first-anniversary-registrations-growth).

The shape of that year matters as much as the total. A brand that has done most of its volume in the last quarter is a brand that has just finished building its network, and 80 sites is the point at which a manufacturer can sell to people who are not prepared to drive an hour for a service. Geely says it has signed the contract for its 100th location and has a longer term plan for 130 to 140 dealers.

The service question is the one a new brand has to answer in year two. A customer who bought in the first year is approaching their first service, and the parts supply and technician training behind a network of 80 sites is what determines whether that experience matches the one they had in the showroom. Geely has not published workshop numbers or its roadside assistance arrangements, and those are the details that tend to arrive with the second year of sales rather than the first.

The Coventry headquarters is part of the same argument. An office with 92 people in it, in a city with an automotive skills base, is a statement that the brand intends to stay rather than run the UK from a distributor's office in another country. Whether that survives the first bad year is the test every new entrant faces.

"Our first year has been one that we can be incredibly proud of as a team as we have achieved so much," said Michael Yang, managing director of Geely Auto UK.

## The product plan

The brand arrived with the EX5 electric SUV and now sells three models in Britain. Three more are expected in 2027. Geely has not published UK prices or specifications for those, and the anniversary material does not name them, so the near term plan is a broader range rather than a change of technology.

The 2027 target is 45,000 UK registrations, more than double the 2026 goal. That is the company's own figure and it is the number to watch, because it implies the brand expects to more than double again on the back of a network that is only just complete.

## The arithmetic of the next year

The arithmetic is worth doing. The 20,000 target is for the 2026 calendar year, and by 23 October the brand had registered 15,264 cars since launch. Hitting the target therefore depends on holding something close to its September rate of 5,155 registrations a month, which is a run rate it reached only once and only after the network was complete.

The 2027 figure of 45,000 is a different order of task. It is nearly three times everything the brand has sold in Britain so far, and it depends on the three additional models arriving on schedule and on the network reaching the 130 to 140 sites the company has described as its long term plan.

Fleet deals matter to that arithmetic as much as retail. The company cites agreements with Tusker and Octopus, and a single fleet contract can move a monthly total in a way that a handful of showroom sales cannot. That is also where the risk sits, because fleet demand is more sensitive to residual values than private demand, and a new brand's residuals are unproven until the first cars reach the used market in volume.

## What it says about the wider market

Chinese brands have been taking a growing share of UK registrations all year, and the pattern has been a slow build rather than a sudden arrival. We have covered that shift in [our piece on the record share](/chinese-brands-record-share/), and the dealer groups that carry these brands have been reporting the effect on their own numbers, as [Vertu's interim results showed](/vertu-interim-results/).

The next phase for these brands is the one Geely is entering now: moving from a launch story to a volume business, where the cars are bought by people who are not early adopters. That requires a service network, competitive finance and a resale value the trade will underwrite, and none of those are settled by a good first year.

For buyers, the practical question about any new brand remains the same. The car is only part of the purchase, and the dealer network, the parts supply and the warranty support are what a five-year ownership experience actually depends on.
