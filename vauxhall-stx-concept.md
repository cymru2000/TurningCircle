Vauxhall published details of the STX Concept on 11 October: a design study for an all-electric estate that [goes on public display at the Paris Motor Show on Monday](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/vauxhall/press/vauxhall-reveals-future-estate-design-with-new-stx-concept). The name stands for Sports Tourer eXperimental, and the car is doing two jobs at once. Only one of them comes with a date attached.

The first job is an argument: that the estate body still has a future in a market that has spent years buying taller cars. The second is a rebrand. The STX is the first Vauxhall to wear a redrawn Griffin emblem, one that drops the flag from a badge that has carried it for more than 120 years, and that emblem is confirmed for the company's next all-new production model in 2027.

The badge is the promise. The estate is the pitch. Vauxhall has said the first will reach showrooms and has not said the same about the second.

## The company with the standing to try

Vauxhall's claim on the estate is not a marketing invention. The company says it [introduced the Victor F Estate in 1958](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/vauxhall/press/vauxhall-announces-new-visionary-stx-concept-car), which it describes as the start of a long line of factory-built estates, and the line runs unbroken to the current Astra Sports Tourer. That car is quoted at [up to 1,634 litres of load volume](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/vauxhall/press/vauxhall-announces-new-visionary-stx-concept-car) with a choice of electric, plug-in hybrid, hybrid and petrol power.

Against that heritage sits the market. In September, [six of the ten best-selling new cars in Britain were SUVs or crossovers](https://www.smmt.co.uk/vehicle-data/car-registrations/): the Jaecoo 7 at the top on 10,814, then the Ford Puma, Kia Sportage, Tesla Model Y, Nissan Qashqai and MG HS. The only Vauxhall in the top ten was the Corsa, seventh on 5,498, and the Corsa is a supermini. No estate appears in that list at all.

The case for the shape is not nostalgia, though. A low roof and a long tail present less frontal area and let the air leave more gently than a tall box of the same length, which is why the release treats aerodynamics as engineering rather than decoration. The company's own phrasing is that the STX "reimagines the electric estate car". The efficiency argument is what makes that more than a styling exercise: range comes from drag as well as from battery cells.

## A Griffin without a flag

The emblem change is the part with a confirmed future. Vauxhall describes the new mark as sharper and more streamlined, keeping the Griffin's essential character while introducing a cleaner, more angular design, and [removing the flag for the first time](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/vauxhall/press/vauxhall-reveals-future-estate-design-with-new-stx-concept) in a lineage of more than 120 years. It is shaped to read clearly when it is lit at the centre of the Vizor, and it will appear on the next all-new Vauxhall production model, which the company says will be revealed in 2027.

A badge is the cheapest thing a car company can change and the easiest to get wrong. Taking the flag off a mark that has carried one for more than a century is a bigger decision than the size of the change suggests, and it signals that the next generation of cars is meant to look like a departure rather than a facelift. The 2027 car will settle whether that reading is fair.

## What is actually on the car

The front end is the clearest statement. The Vizor is taller and more prominent than on current Vauxhalls, and its lighting signature uses angled vertical elements that form the outer edges of the grille area before fading towards the centre. The illuminated Griffin sits inside the Vauxhall Compass, the vertical and horizontal axes the brand uses as its face, and the bonnet crease is emphasised by a sharper front section.

The rear slopes steeply. Vauxhall makes a practical claim for that as well as a visual one, saying the profile adds load space, and it repeats the Compass motif with slim illuminated axes and an illuminated wordmark at the centre. Glass light strips run rearwards from the window line at the C-pillars and continue into the roof, and illuminated STX lettering along the flanks tapers towards the nose. Cameras replace the door mirrors, which is a familiar show-car move and still rare on cars you can buy.

The body is finished in silver and blue, with short overhangs and pronounced wheelarches. It rides on 285/35 R22 Goodyear tyres, and the three-section alloy wheels are a deliberate reference to the Nova and the first Corsa, which Vauxhall says wore similar designs.

Then there is the moving bodywork. The STX has an active aerodynamic system that adjusts to conditions, with blue-coloured diffusers extending from beneath the rear at speed and vertical elements behind the wheels moving outwards to smooth the flow along the flanks. Vauxhall frames all of this as a way to cut consumption and add range, which is the most plausible reason for a show car to have it.

The one performance number in the release is a top speed of more than 124mph, quoted alongside all-wheel drive. There is no power output, no battery capacity and no range figure, which is normal for a concept and the reason not to make much of the 124mph. A show car's top speed tells you what the company wants the car to feel like, not what it will do.

## The cabin is described in the future tense

Vauxhall's interior paragraph is written as a plan rather than a description. The release says that "in the future, the designers and engineers will realise an interior" that develops the Painting with Light approach used on the 2023 Vauxhall Experimental and the 2025 Corsa GSE Vision Gran Turismo, with lightweight seats and immersive lighting.

Read that carefully and it says the show car has no finished cabin to report on. The interior is a stated intention, and any coverage that describes materials and screens in the present tense is describing something Vauxhall has not shown.

## What Vauxhall has not said

There is no production confirmation for an electric estate. There is no price, no battery size, no range, no power figure and no on-sale date for the STX or anything like it. The only date in the release belongs to the 2027 all-new model that will wear the new badge, and Vauxhall does not say that car is an estate, a crossover or anything else.

Vauxhall has built a convincing argument that the estate shape deserves a place in an electric range, and it has put that argument in front of the public at a show full of tall cars. It has not committed to building one.

## The market it would land in

The September numbers show a market with room for new shapes and a strong preference for familiar ones. [Registrations rose 12.0% to 350,536 cars](https://www.smmt.co.uk/record-electric-car-market-powers-bumper-september/), the tenth consecutive month of growth, and battery electric cars took a record 28.3% share on 99,201 units.

What Vauxhall does not have is volume above the supermini. The Corsa carries the brand, and the growth in the market is coming from Chinese entrants and from small electric cars, as our [September share coverage](/chinese-brands-record-share/) set out. An electric estate would be a niche product in a market where the money is in crossovers. That is the commercial case against, and Vauxhall has not answered it.

## What to watch

Two things. The 2027 production model will show how much of this design language survives contact with a showroom and a price list. The other is whether the aero hardware, the extending diffusers and the camera mirrors, turns up on something with a registration plate.

The [full September registration picture](/september-2026-registrations-record/) explains why the brand needs a stronger story above the Corsa. Whether that story turns out to be an estate is still open, and the concept is the opening argument rather than the answer.
