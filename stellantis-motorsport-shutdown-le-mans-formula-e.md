Stellantis is preparing to shut down its motorsport division, a move that would end Peugeot's Le Mans programme, the Vauxhall and Opel Formula E entries and the reborn Lancia rally effort, according to a report in France's Le Figaro newspaper on Friday ([Reuters, 20 November](https://www.reuters.com/business/stellantis-brands-exit-motorsports-reports-le-figaro-2026-11-20/); [Autocar, 20 November](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/stellantis-end-all-motorsport-investment-%E2%80%93-report)). The decision is due to be formally announced on Monday after an extraordinary meeting of the group's works councils, Stellantis confirmed, though it said it could not comment further while the consultation process runs. The news matters beyond the racing world: Stellantis is the parent of Vauxhall, and the closure would shut the Opel Formula E entry before it has raced, leaving Britain's biggest-selling van maker with no factory motorsport presence at a time when it is rebuilding its UK identity around the Ellesmere Port plant.

## What is being closed

Stellantis Motorsport costs the group around €300 million, roughly £251 million, a year to run, the report says. Closing it would end the Peugeot 9X8 programme in the World Endurance Championship, which includes the 24 Hours of Le Mans. The hypercar was launched in 2022 and has not won a race since, and it was being reworked for the 2027 season when the reports emerged. The Opel Formula E entry, announced in March this year on a four-year contract with the Gen4 cars, would also stop, along with the Citroen team that took over Maserati's Formula E entry in 2025 and is likely to continue under MSG Racing with a new sponsor. Lancia's return to the World Rally Championship, which began in the WRC2 class this year and has already notched wins with the petrol Ypsilon Rally2 HF Integrale, is another programme expected to be cut. Customer racing teams would still be supported. Peugeot finished seventh in this year's WEC standings, the second season running, and has not won a race since returning with the 9X8 in 2022 ([RaceFans, 20 November](https://www.racefans.net/2026/11/20/spotlight-report-claims-stellantis-will-cancel-its-peugeot-wec-and-citroen-formula-e-programmes/)).

## Why now

The cost sits against a difficult set of numbers for the group's home region. In its third-quarter results, published on 28 October, Stellantis reported an adjusted operating loss of €311 million in Enlarged Europe, against a €157 million profit a year earlier, a swing of €468 million, on net revenues of €14.1 billion, up 3% ([Stellantis, 28 October](https://www.stellantis.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/october/third-quarter-2026-financial-results)). Enlarged Europe shipments rose 10% to 588,000 units in the quarter, but revenue per vehicle fell, which the group attributed to negative net pricing and an unfavourable mix. Group net revenues rose 1% to €37.6 billion and adjusted operating income improved to €256 million, but Europe was the only region in the red, and CEO Antonio Filosa told investors: "[Europe] remains challenging, with ongoing Chinese competition and a difficult pricing environment" ([Autocar, 20 November](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/stellantis-end-all-motorsport-investment-%E2%80%93-report)). The group is targeting €6 billion of savings under its Value Creation Program by 2028, and motorsport is a visible discretionary spend. At the same earnings call, Filosa reaffirmed the 2026 guidance, including positive industrial free cash flow in 2027, after the group burned €4.0 billion of industrial cash in the quarter.

## What it means for UK brands

For Vauxhall, the report marks the end of the Opel-branded Formula E programme before it has raced, a setback for a brand that has leaned on performance imagery as it climbed back up the UK sales charts. The closure would leave no British-based Stellantis entry in top-level international motorsport. It also lands in the same week that Vauxhall began fitting the official Made in Britain badge to its Ellesmere Port-built Combo Electric vans, a sign that the group's UK strategy is now anchored in van output at the UK's first electric-only plant rather than in racing. The plant employs more than 1,000 people and also builds the Citroen Berlingo, Peugeot Partner and Fiat Doblò electric vans on the same line. The wider picture for the brand this month is mixed: our coverage of [the 82,000 Vauxhall cars still under a Takata stop-drive order](/vauxhall-takata-stop-drive-82000-cars/) shows the recall burden still running through the UK fleet, while [electric van registrations and the mandate gap](/electric-van-record-mandate-gap-doubles/) frame the output the Ellesmere Port line is being asked to deliver.

## The wider pattern

Stellantis is not the only manufacturer cutting back. Alpine announced earlier this year that it would end its World Endurance Championship programme at the end of the 2026 season, and Porsche pulled out of the series in 2025 while remaining in Formula E. If Peugeot confirms its exit, the WEC's top class would be left without a French manufacturer, following Alpine's departure. The reports also note that efforts are underway to offload at least one of the Formula E programmes to a Chinese partner ([RACER, 20 November](https://racer.com/2026/11/20/peugeot-s-wec-program-facing-the-axe-ahead-of-2027-season)).

## What happens next

Stellantis has called extraordinary meetings of the CSE and CSEC works councils for Monday 23 November to discuss the proposals with employee representatives, and French law requires that consultation to run before any structural change is made. Peugeot has confirmed the meetings but declined to give any indication of the topics, saying only that it "cannot provide any indication about these topics to respect the information and the consultation process". The deadline for submitting entries to the 2027 FIA World Endurance Championship falls on the same day, which is why the timing of the reports matters to rival teams and organisers. Until the works council process concludes and Stellantis makes a formal announcement, the shutdown remains a report rather than a confirmed decision, and the group's statement to date is limited to confirmation of the meetings.
