TVR has named a factory for the new Griffith at last. The British sports car maker's owner, MOTV Group, announced on Friday that the long-delayed V8 coupe will be developed and built at Factory 3, part of Lotus's Hethel Performance Hub in Norfolk, with dynamic testing starting imminently and first customer deliveries planned for 2028. It is the first revival attempt to put a production site, a test track and a funding plan behind the promise, rather than just a rendering.

The new Griffith keeps the formula of the car unveiled at the Goodwood Revival in September 2017: a [bespoke lightweight spaceframe chassis with a composite body](https://tvr.co.uk/), a V8 engine of around 500bhp, a manual gearbox and rear-wheel drive. The starting price is expected to sit near £120,000, the 2017 figure of about £90,000 adjusted for inflation, according to [This is Money's report of the announcement](https://www.thisismoney.co.uk/money/cars/article-16324767/TVR-returns-Iconic-British-sports-car-firm-build-reborn-120-000-Griffith-Lotuss-Hethel-factory.html), which puts it in Porsche 911 Carrera S territory.

## Why this attempt is different

TVR has promised a new Griffith before and missed. The 2017 car was due to be built in a factory in Ebbw Vale, South Wales, in a planned run of 500 Launch Edition cars priced under £90,000, but the Welsh funding collapsed, the iStream chassis process was lost when Gordon Murray Technologies was sold, and no production car ever reached a customer. [evo's account of the saga](https://evo.co.uk/tvr/207999/tvrs-comeback-continues-lotus-to-help-deliver-griffith-by-2028) traces the chain from the Ebbw Vale plan through the Dublin listing to the current ownership.

MOTV Group, the specialist UK automotive group formerly known as Charge Holdings, took a controlling stake in TVR in November 2025 and has since taken full operational control, installing a new board led by chief executive Paul Abercrombie. The group says it has a funding plan backed by private equity investors and a restructured, relatively debt-free balance sheet, and that the Lotus arrangement is a multi-year agreement with room to expand. The production line at Hethel currently has capacity for about five cars a week, with a ceiling of 1,000 cars a year, [Autonext reports](https://www.autonext.co.uk/news/tvr-griffith-lotus-hethel-production-2028), a cap that keeps TVR inside the low-volume manufacturer rules rather than exposing it to mainstream fleet-average emissions targets.

## What Hethel gives the project

Factory 3 is the hall at the Hethel Performance Hub that Lotus opened in June 2026 to let other manufacturers use its site. [The Manufacturer's report on the hub's launch](https://www.themanufacturer.com/articles/lotus-launches-hethel-performance-hub-to-accelerate-uk-automotive-innovation/) notes it was created less than a year after Lotus announced plans to cut up to 550 jobs at Hethel, and that the shared model is designed to keep the Norfolk site viable by building cars for outside brands. Zenos, the British lightweight maker we covered when its E10 returned in [October](/zenos-e10-returns/), is the other named tenant, so the Griffith will be built alongside another low-volume British sports car rather than in a factory of its own.

For TVR the practical gain is immediate: its own production space, access to Lotus's engineering and development facilities, and the Hethel test track on the doorstep. Paul Abercrombie, CEO of TVR and MOTV Group, said: "Hethel gives TVR a proper home, with its own factory and a test track on the doorstep, right at the heart of British sports car engineering."

## The history behind the name

TVR was founded in Blackpool by Trevor Wilkinson, whose engineering business became TVR Engineering and whose first name supplied the consonants for the brand. [TVR's own heritage pages](https://www.tvr.co.uk/heritage/wilkinson-era) record that the first chassis was built in 1949 and that the original Griffith ran from 1990 to 2002 under Peter Wheeler, who also gave the company the Cerbera, Tuscan and Sagaris. Production at the Blackpool factory ended in 2006, and the brand turns 80 next year. The 2028 Griffith is effectively an updated version of the 2017 car, with refreshed styling and interior promised for a full reveal in 2027; left-hand-drive versions homologated for the US market are planned for later in 2028, [Autoweek reports](https://www.autoweek.com/news/a74543655/new-tvr-griffith-coming-to-us-in-2028/), which would be TVR's first American-market car since 1987.

## What to watch between now and 2028

The honest caveat is that a factory announcement is not a car. The 2017 project also had deposits, a claimed £40 million of orders and a sold-out first run on paper, and depositors were later refunded. What is genuinely new this time is the combination of a named production site with spare capacity, an existing test track, and an owner that says it has already restructured the business. None of that guarantees the 2028 date: the engine supplier and final Cosworth involvement remain unconfirmed, production volumes are unstated, and dynamic testing has yet to begin. For UK buyers, though, the Hethel link ties the Griffith to the same manufacturing postcode as [October's production figures](/smmt-october-production-2026/), where the SMMT showed British factories building more for export than for home - a reminder of how small and specialist the domestic sports car industry has become.
