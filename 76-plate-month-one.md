A month has passed since the 76 plate arrived, and the number everyone wants does not exist yet. The SMMT publishes September's registration data in early October, so any figure for the plate month you may have seen quoted is a forecast, not a result. What we can do honestly at the one-month mark is read the data that has landed around the plate change: August's market, the used market's response, and the auction figures that show how dealers and buyers behaved while September did its work.

## Where the market stood going in

August gave the 76-plate month a running start. The SMMT recorded [94,236 new car registrations in August, up 13.7% and the ninth consecutive month of market growth](https://www.smmt.co.uk/evs-lift-august-new-car-market-but-september-is-key-test/), the best August since the biannual plate change was introduced. Battery electric cars took 29.8% of the month, the second-highest monthly share of 2026, though the SMMT itself cautions that August is a low-volume month where percentages get amplified, and that [BEV uptake for the first eight months stands at a record 25.6%](https://www.smmt.co.uk/evs-lift-august-new-car-market-but-september-is-key-test/), still short of the 33% mandate target for the year.

Mike Hawes, the SMMT's chief executive, put the frame for the month ahead plainly: "August is a low-volume month, so September will be the acid test." That is exactly the test whose result we are all still waiting for. It is also the test the industry has spent the month preparing for, with plate-change offers stacking on top of the Electric Car Grant and dealers holding stock back for the 1 September rush.

## What the used market was doing while September ran

The plate change works on the used market as much as the new one, and the auction data is the clearest window into it. Dealer Auction's EV Performance Review for August, published on 22 September, shows the plate-change effect on buyer behaviour: [some retail customers deliberately wait until after the plate change to see what part-exchange stock becomes available](https://www.dealerauction.co.uk/ev-performance-review/evpr-august-2026/), while others buy before it. The review also recorded [the Nissan Qashqai topping the volume-sold chart for a fourth consecutive month](https://www.dealerauction.co.uk/ev-performance-review/evpr-august-2026/), a reminder that the cars moving in greatest numbers around a plate change are the ordinary family staples, not the headline acts.

The same data shows the stock flowing in is getting newer and fresher: average age dropped from 6.9 years to 5 years, and average mileage fell from 68,050 to 49,240. For used buyers, the weeks after a plate change are historically when part-exchange stock from new registrations starts reaching forecourts, which is a pattern worth remembering whether or not this September beats records.

## The used EV market has its own headline

The other reason the one-month picture is worth reading now: the demand side of the used EV market keeps setting records independent of the plate. We covered this in our [used EV parity check](/used-ev-parity-check/) published today, but the short version is that [the Tesla Model Y set a new record as the fastest-selling used EV ever tracked in the review, at 21 days](https://www.dealerauction.co.uk/ev-performance-review/evpr-august-2026/), and used electric cars broadly are selling faster than the wider used market for a third consecutive month. Record used demand matters for the September read because it supports the values of the cars being part-exchanged against new 76-plate purchases, which in turn supports the new market itself. A healthy trade-in price makes a new car easier to justify, so the two halves of the market are currently reinforcing each other rather than pulling apart.

## What September's numbers will decide

When the SMMT publishes in early October, three questions get answers. First, volume: September traditionally carries a large share of the year's registrations, so the total will confirm or puncture the growth story built over nine straight months. Second, the BEV share at full volume: 29.8% in a quiet August is a strong signal, but the mandate arithmetic runs on the year, and the first-eight-months figure of 25.6% against a 33% target means the plate month needed to overperform. Third, private demand: fleet buyers are steered by tax and regulation, while the private share of a plate month shows what ordinary households chose with their own money.

The AA's quarterly index, published on 23 September, gives a useful sense of the mood going into the read: its [EV Readiness Index hit 60 out of 100, the highest recorded, with new EVs still 25% dearer than petrol equivalents](https://www.theaa.com/about-us/newsroom/AA-UK-EV-readiness-index-September-2026). The market is readier than it has ever been and still not ready, which is precisely the tension the September figures will quantify.

## The honest read

There is a temptation, one month after a plate change, to declare what it meant. The honest position is that the supporting data points one way, August was strong, used EV demand is at record levels and accelerating, dealers reported buyers coming off the fence once part-exchange stock appeared, but the number that settles the argument is a few weeks away. Our [76-plate preview](/the-76-plate-preview/) set out what to watch before the month began and [day one](/the-76-plate-day-one/) covered the mechanics; when the SMMT publishes early next month, the headline share, the volume and the private-versus-fleet split will turn this month of anticipation into a verdict. Until then, treat any September registration figure you see as a guess dressed as news.
