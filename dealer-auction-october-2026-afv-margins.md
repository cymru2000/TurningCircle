Used hybrids led Dealer Auction's October margin rankings, with the hybrid BMW X5 delivering an average potential margin of £8,200 and the average across alternative fuel vehicles (AFVs) rising to £3,296, up almost 37% since January ([Dealer Auction EV Performance Review, 19 November](https://www.dealerauction.co.uk/ev-performance-review/evpr-october-2026/)). Hybrids passed 9% of platform stock for the first time this year.

The electric side of the table was quieter on margin but quick on speed. The Volkswagen ID.3 sold in 27 days and the Nissan Leaf in 29.2, putting both among October's fastest sellers, while the hybrid Nissan Qashqai was the best-selling AFV for the sixth consecutive month ([AM Online, 20 November](https://www.am-online.com/news/hybrids-lead-used-car-margins-as-two-evs-sell-in-under-30-days)).

## The hybrid X5 took the top margin spot

The hybrid BMW X5 topped the AFV margin chart for the third time in 2026, with an average potential margin of £8,200 in October ([Dealer Auction, 19 November](https://www.dealerauction.co.uk/ev-performance-review/evpr-october-2026/)). That sits just below the £8,520 the Volvo XC60 recorded in March, the highest margin the monthly review has logged. Dealer Auction calculates the margin as the difference between the wholesale purchase price and the retail market value, so the table shows where a dealer can still make money rather than which cars move fastest.

Nine of the ten highest-margin models were SUVs. The BMW 3 Series, in fifth place, was the only exception. Volvo's XC90, XC60 and XC40 hybrids all held places in the top ten, and the hybrid Qashqai was the best-selling AFV for the sixth month in a row, the longest uninterrupted run the review records.

## The average AFV margin is up 37% since January

Across all AFVs, the average potential margin reached £3,296 in October, slightly above September and part of a run of months above £3,000. The figure has risen by £884 since January, an increase of almost 37% ([Dealer Auction, 19 November](https://www.dealerauction.co.uk/ev-performance-review/evpr-october-2026/)).

The mix of stock shifted as well. Hybrids accounted for more than 9% of everything on the platform in October, their highest share of 2026 and up from 7.71% in September. EVs and hybrids together made up their strongest combined share of stock since May, a sign that alternative-fuelled cars are steadily feeding through from the new-car market.

## Two EVs sold in under 30 days

The Nissan Leaf ranked fourth by volume among AFVs and recorded the strongest performance against CAP Clean, with the Volkswagen ID.3 second on that measure ([AM Online, 20 November](https://www.am-online.com/news/hybrids-lead-used-car-margins-as-two-evs-sell-in-under-30-days)). On speed to sale, a metric covering cars sold naturally through digital auction, the ID.3 took 27 days and the Leaf 29.2 days, both among the fastest sellers of the month.

September's review had shown a similar pattern in different clothes: the Volvo XC90 led that month's margin table at £6,272, with the Tesla Model Y the only EV in the top ten ([Fleet News, 22 October](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/average-age-of-used-evs-and-hybrids-coming-down-dealer-auction-says)). October's table was more heavily hybrid and SUV-led, but the direction of travel was the same.

## AFV stock is getting younger and lower-mileage

Average AFV mileage on the platform fell for the third consecutive month, to 34,074 miles, more than 10% below September's 38,100 and well under the 68,050 recorded in July ([Dealer Auction, 19 November](https://www.dealerauction.co.uk/ev-performance-review/evpr-october-2026/)). The average age was 4.9 years, against 4.7 in September ([Fleet News, 22 October](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/average-age-of-used-evs-and-hybrids-coming-down-dealer-auction-says)).

Younger, lower-mileage stock reaching the trade is the expected echo of September's plate change feeding part-exchanges through to auction. TeeBoon noted the same effect a month earlier, saying the September new-car surge was already showing in the age and mileage of AFVs coming to market.

## The retail side tells a similar story

Autotrader's October Retail Price Index shows the retail market holding up even as trade values slipped. The average asking price for a used car was £17,321, flat month on month and down 1.8% on a year earlier, while cars sold in an average of 29 days ([Autotrader, 9 November](https://plc.autotrader.co.uk/news-views/press-releases/used-car-retail-prices-hold-steady-month-on-month-as-market-continues-to-show-resilience/)). Electric cars recorded a sixth consecutive month of annual price growth, up 2.8% to an average of £25,263, and were the fastest-selling fuel type at 25 days, four days quicker than petrol. Demand for used EVs grew 38.1% on a year earlier against an 11.5% rise in supply.

That retail strength lines up with the auction data. Our October reports on BCA's average sale price of £7,244 ([/auction-values-october-2026/]) and cap hpi's 1.7% like-for-like fall ([/used-values-october-2026/]) describe a softer wholesale market, while EVs and hybrids are the fuel types holding their value and their speed.

## What it means for dealers and buyers

Kieran TeeBoon, marketplace director at Dealer Auction, said: "As we enter the final months of 2026, tracking the bidding activity on AFVs will help provide a more rounded picture, helping dealers identify vehicles that offer genuine potential rather than simply following the busiest parts of the market." He added: "It's brilliant to see the resilience of the UK automotive market and continued growth of the used AFV segment against a backdrop of headlines about ongoing economic pressures."

The new-car market is feeding that growth. EVs and plug-in hybrids together took 59% of the 152,730 cars registered in October, with battery cars alone at 32.0% ([SMMT, 5 November](https://www.smmt.co.uk/year-end-push-lifts-ev-sales-but-mandate-gap-set-to-widen)). Those cars reach the used market in three to four years, which is why dealers are watching AFV bidding activity now.

For anyone stocking a forecourt, the October table offers two different opportunities. Premium hybrid SUVs such as the X5 and the Volvo models are where the margin sits, while selected electric hatchbacks such as the ID.3 and the Leaf are where the speed sits. The two groups rarely overlap, which is what makes the split worth tracking.
