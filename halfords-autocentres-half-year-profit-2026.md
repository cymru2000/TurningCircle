Halfords has reported a sharp rise in garage profits in its half-year results, as the group's 497 consumer garages step up their challenge to franchised dealers for servicing and repair work. Autocentres operating profit rose from £5.7m to £15m in the 26 weeks to 2 October 2026, while division revenue grew 5.9% to £381.3m ([AM Online, 27 November](https://www.am-online.com/news/halfords-autocentres-operating-profit-more-than-doubles-to-15m)).

The wider group turned over £953.3m in the half year, up 6.7%, and lifted underlying pre-tax profit 66% to £39.0m, raising full-year guidance to between £60m and £65m ([Halfords interim results, 26 November](https://halfordscompany.com/media/55whlxu3/fy27-interim-results-announcement-vf.pdf)). Reported pre-tax profit more than doubled to £35.5m from £17.2m, and the interim dividend rose 16.7% to 3.5p a share.

## Why the workshop is suddenly the profit engine

The profit line matters more than the sales line here. Excluding the loss-making Avayler software business, Autocentres underlying operating profit rose 62.3% to £16.1m and the operating margin climbed from 2.8% to 4.2%, against a medium-term target of 5% to 6% ([Halfords, 26 November](https://halfordscompany.com/media/55whlxu3/fy27-interim-results-announcement-vf.pdf)). The company estimates around £2m of that uplift came from warm weather driving air conditioning demand, but even stripped of that the division showed clear progress towards the target.

Two structural changes sit behind the rest. First, the Fusion programme: 121 of the group's garages were trading under the upgraded model at the half-year, with the remainder due to complete in the second half. Converted garages typically double garage-level profit, Halfords says. Second, changes to technician scheduling cut labour costs by more than two percentage points as a share of sales across the consumer garage network, and tablets in the bays let technicians show customers the extra work identified and secure approval for it on the spot ([AM Online, 27 November](https://www.am-online.com/news/halfords-autocentres-operating-profit-more-than-doubles-to-15m)).

The half-year also carried a one-off tailwind. The group estimates the warm summer added about £8m of profit across the business, roughly £2m of it in Autocentres through air conditioning servicing, and it is reinvesting part of that outperformance in the second half ([Halfords, 26 November](https://halfordscompany.com/media/55whlxu3/fy27-interim-results-announcement-vf.pdf)). Group free cash flow reached £38.0m and net cash more than doubled to £40.1m, which is what funds the garage programme without touching the balance sheet. The group's longer-term plan targets a 5% to 6% operating margin in both divisions, and the first major project under its "Evolve" phase is a new national distribution centre expected to deliver low to mid-teens £m of annual benefit at maturity.

## The market Halfords is chasing

The strategy is aimed at what Halfords calls the "Do It For Me" market: motorists who prefer to pay for maintenance and repairs rather than do the work themselves. The company estimates that audience covers almost 20 million vehicles, 59% of the relevant UK parc, up around 30% over the past decade, and puts the wider servicing opportunity at £8bn ([AM Online, 22 September](https://www.am-online.com/news/halfords-steps-up-dealer-challenge-in-8bn-services-market)). Its own annual report puts the total motoring services market at £17bn, shared between Halfords, Kwik Fit, other chains, dealers and independents, with motering services now representing over half of group revenue ([Halfords annual report 2026](https://halfordscompany.com/media/15obfvkz/halfords-group-plc-annual-report-and-accounts-2026-accessible.pdf)).

That is the same battleground [Vertu's interim results](/vertu-interim-results/) put on display in October: there, the workshop generated £129.2m of the group's £290.6m gross profit, more than 44%, on just £223.6m of revenue. Dealer aftersales and independent garages have always competed for this work; the difference now is that a national retail brand is investing in garage capacity to take a bigger slice. The group's own figures show the scale of the network: 366 Halfords stores, 497 consumer garages, 83 commercial fleet locations and roughly 280 mobile service vans ([Halfords, 26 November](https://halfordscompany.com/media/55whlxu3/fy27-interim-results-announcement-vf.pdf)).

## Competition is widening beyond the forecourt

The angle is not unique to Halfords. [What actually fails an MOT](/what-actually-fails-an-mot-2025-26/) this week put the 2025/26 test base at 33.3m car MOTs with a 27.51% initial failure rate, which is the raw demand behind every workshop bay. And on the used side, [November's trade values](/used-values-november-2026/) fell 3.5% at the three-year, 60,000-mile point, so owners are holding on to cars for longer, and older cars need more servicing. Halfords' bet is that this work goes to whoever can offer capacity, booking and price transparency at scale, rather than to the nearest dealer.

## What it means for drivers

For motorists the practical effect is more competition for servicing, MOT and tyre work, including at the 83 commercial fleet locations and roughly 280 mobile service vans in the network, all bookable through a single online platform ([Halfords, 26 November](https://halfordscompany.com/media/55whlxu3/fy27-interim-results-announcement-vf.pdf)). Halfords said October and November trading had returned to more typical growth rates after the summer, and it plans to spend £40m to £50m on a new national distribution centre by FY2030 to support the garage network, with £35m to £45m of one-off costs over three years.

The group also flagged cost pressure. Chief executive Henry Birch told the Press Association the group had already raised some prices in the first half and could not rule out more if energy and fuel costs keep rising ([Yahoo Finance / ADVFN, 26 November](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/halfords-reports-66-rise-half-082624809.html)). The Avayler software arm, meanwhile, saw its operating loss widen from £1.8m to £2.3m and is under review ([AM Online, 27 November](https://www.am-online.com/news/halfords-autocentres-operating-profit-more-than-doubles-to-15m)).
