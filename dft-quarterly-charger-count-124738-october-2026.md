The Department for Transport has published its first quarterly count of public EV chargers as sockets rather than cabinets, putting the UK at [124,738 public chargers on 1 October 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure-statistics-1-october-2026/public-electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure-statistics-1-july-2026), or 180.0 per 100,000 people. That is a record total, but the release, published today, also carries the first official annual comparison: 8,686 chargers added so far in 2026, against 13,281 added across the whole of 2025.

The quarterly series is built from data that charge point operators submit under the Public Charge Point Regulations 2023 and that Zapmap collates for the department. The count switched to chargers, individual sockets, in January, replacing the older device measure, which is why the same release also reports 97,976 public charging devices. The department has said it plans to stop publishing the device metric after February 2027, so from next year the socket count becomes the only official headline.

## The additions slowdown

The 8,686 additions cover the first ten months of the year. Industry estimates quoted in the same release put the full-year additions at 24,557 in 2024 and 20,662 in 2023, figures that sit outside the official series because they predate it. On the official measure, the network is tracking well below last year's pace even though the comparison is partial: 2025's full-year total of 13,281 was reached only at the end of December. We [reported on 11 November](/public-charger-additions-fall-18-percent-2026/) that the monthly faster indicator, which draws on the same Zapmap data, had already shown the slowdown, with 10,088 chargers added in the first ten months against 12,321 over the same period of 2025, a fall of 18%, while electric car registrations rose 30.4% over the same stretch. The quarterly release confirms the direction, and the gap matters because the UK's [record 126,140 chargers at the end of October](/uk-charger-count-passes-126000-rapid-30000/) rest on exactly this installation rate holding up through the winter.

The gap between chargers and cars is still comfortable on paper. As of 1 July 2026 there was one public charger for every 18 battery electric vehicles in the UK, the release states, though that ratio uses July vehicle data because October licensing figures were not yet available. With more than 503,805 battery electric cars registered in the first ten months of the year alone, the ratio is one the department will have to keep refreshing each quarter.

## What sits behind the chargers

By power, 49% of the network is standard chargers rated 3kW to less than 8kW, 27% are standard plus at 8kW to less than 50kW, and 12% each are rapid (50kW to less than 150kW) and ultra-rapid (150kW and above). Rapid or above totals [29,772 chargers](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure-statistics-1-october-2026/public-electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure-statistics-1-july-2026), or 42.9 per 100,000 people. By location, 52% of chargers are classed as destination, 33% on-street, 11% en-route and 4% other. In England, 9,616 rapid or above chargers sit within one mile of the Strategic Road Network, of which 8,857 are open-access.

Geography still skews. London has far more chargers per capita than anywhere else yet the second-lowest rate of rapid or above provision, because so much of its stock is standard on-street units. In England and Wales, 16.8% of public chargers are in rural areas, against 17.5% of the population living there.

## The grant side is thinner still

The companion grant release in today's publication shows how little of the network is publicly funded. Home charging grant schemes have funded 413,420 installations since 2013, but only 8,166 in the last 12 months, and workplace schemes have funded 88,145 since 2016 with 11,517 in the last 12 months. Public charging grants have funded a provisional 25,858 installations since 2017, comprising 2,153 funded by the LEVI Fund in England as of 1 October and 23,705 sockets across completed ORCS projects. The cross-pavement on-street grant for households has funded just [82 socket installations in total, of which 44 in the last 12 months, for £30,778](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure-statistics-1-october-2026/grant-schemes-for-electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure-statistics-1-july-2026), a scheme aimed at exactly the households that [our reporting on on-street charging](/on-street-charging-councils/) and the [charger sharing campaign](/charger-sharing-campaign-9-million-households/) have identified as the hardest to serve.

The older EVHS home scheme, now closed, funded 340,222 domestic installations for £140.8 million, and the Workplace Charging Scheme has funded 74,097 sockets for £27.7 million since late 2016. The renter and flat-owner chargepoint grant has funded 27,208 sockets for £9.6 million, and the landlord scheme a further 1,919 for £677,483. The department notes that the most recent months of grant data are undercounted because applications take months to process, and that the LEVI figure for the latest quarter is a provisional estimate subject to revision.

## Why it matters for drivers

The charger total is the number that gets quoted, but the release's own caveats matter for anyone relying on the network. The grant figures are provisional, the latest months are undercounted, and privately funded home chargers are invisible to the statistics entirely. Meanwhile the pace of new public chargers is running behind last year even as [electric cars took 32.0% of October registrations](/electric-cars-32-percent-october/), and the department's plan to retire the device count in February 2027 means the socket series becomes the single official measure from next year. For drivers without off-street parking, the 33% on-street share and the 82-socket cross-pavement grant total are the figures that describe their actual options.
